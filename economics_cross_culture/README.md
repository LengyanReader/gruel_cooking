# economics_cross_culture — Cross-Culture Economics

An independent directory collecting cases, principles, and concepts from various economic traditions across cultures.

## Purpose

Different civilizations developed distinct economic thought in response to their own material conditions, political structures, and philosophical traditions. By placing these traditions side by side, we can:

- Identify universal economic principles that appear independently across cultures
- Understand how institutional context shapes economic behavior
- Find blind spots in any single tradition by comparing with others
- Build a more complete picture of how economies actually work

## Subdirectories

- `western/` — Western economic traditions (classical, neoclassical, Austrian, Keynesian, institutional, etc.)
- `chinese/` — Chinese economic thought (中国传统经济思想: fiscal systems, agricultural economics, guanxi economics, statecraft)
- `other_traditions/` — Other cultural economic thought (Islamic, Indian, African, indigenous, etc.)
- `comparative/` — Cross-tradition analysis: where traditions agree, diverge, or illuminate each other
- `cases/` — Specific historical cases that illustrate economic principles across cultures

## Record Format

Each entry includes:
- Tradition or school of thought
- Core principles and key thinkers
- Historical context: what problem was this tradition trying to solve?
- Key texts and primary sources
- Comparison with other traditions
- Connection to first principles in `/core`
