# western — Western Economic Traditions

Major schools of Western economic thought, from ancient Greece to modern economics.

## School Categories

### Classical Economics
- Adam Smith — *The Wealth of Nations* (1776), division of labor, invisible hand
- David Ricardo — comparative advantage, labor theory of value
- John Stuart Mill — synthesis of classical political economy

### Neoclassical Economics
- Marginal revolution: Jevons, Menger, Walras
- General equilibrium theory: Arrow-Debreu
- Rational choice and utility maximization

### Austrian Economics
- Carl Menger — subjective value, spontaneous order
- Ludwig von Mises — praxeology, economic calculation problem
- Friedrich Hayek — knowledge problem, price signals

### Keynesian and Post-Keynesian
- John Maynard Keynes — aggregate demand, animal spirits
- Hyman Minsky — financial instability hypothesis
- Modern Monetary Theory (MMT)

### Institutional Economics
- Thorstein Veblen — conspicuous consumption
- Ronald Coase — transaction costs, theorem of the firm
- Douglass North — institutions and economic history

### Behavioral Economics
- Daniel Kahneman & Amos Tversky — prospect theory, cognitive biases
- Richard Thaler — nudge theory, mental accounting

## Record Format

Each school includes:
- Founding figures and key texts
- Core principles and assumptions
- Historical context: what problem was it trying to solve?
- Criticisms and limitations
- Cross-references to other traditions (especially `/chinese/` and `/comparative/`)
