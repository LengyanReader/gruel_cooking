# comparative — Cross-Tradition Analysis

Where different economic traditions agree, diverge, or illuminate each other.

## Analysis Directions

### Universal Principles
- Concepts that appear independently across multiple traditions
- Examples: the role of trust, the problem of scarcity, the function of prices
- Why do these principles recur? Are they truly universal or culturally contingent?

### Divergences
- Where traditions disagree on fundamental questions
- Examples: role of the state vs market, individual vs collective, interest/profit ethics
- What underlying assumptions cause these divergences?

### Blind Spots
- What each tradition fails to see because of its own assumptions
- Western economics: underestimation of relational/ritual dimensions
- Chinese economics: undertheorization of formal institutional design
- Others: varying gaps depending on context

### Cross-Illumination
- Cases where one tradition explains something another tradition takes for granted
- Examples: guanxi economics illuminating Western network theory, Islamic finance illuminating debt ethics

## Record Format

Each analysis includes:
- Traditions being compared
- The specific concept or phenomenon under comparison
- Points of agreement and disagreement
- Underlying assumptions driving the difference
- Synthesis or open question
