第二圈 · 源头与对手（十六册）Ring 2 · sources and rivals (sixteen volumes)

特辑只是这条长河的最近一段。往上游：每个美学关键词都有日文原典与英译通道；「日本是什么」由茶道、武士道、阴翳、徒然草、花传反复作答；「论日本」从黄遵宪、戴季陶到本尼迪克特、加藤周一，再到李永晶的《分身》。文豪侧翼回到荷风、漱石、芥川、川端的原文现场。
The Special is only the nearest reach of one long river. Upstream, each aesthetic keyword has a Japanese original and an English channel; what Japan is gets answered again and again by tea, bushido, shadow, Essays in Idleness and the No treatise; Japan-ology runs Huang Zunxian, Dai Jitao, Benedict and Kato to Li Yongjing's Avatar. The literary wing returns to Kafu, Soseki, Akutagawa and Kawabata.

三条轴 · 三条谱系Three axes · three genealogies

甲 · 思想史 · 谁在论日本intellectual history · who discusses Japan

从志书（黄遵宪）到策论（戴季陶），再到外视定型（本尼迪克特）、日本自评（加藤周一），最后收于中国认识论的反省（李永晶）。
From gazetteer (Huang) to policy tract (Dai), to outside typing (Benedict) and Japanese self-assessment (Kato), closing on a Chinese epistemological reflection (Li).

乙 · 美学 · 范畴的谱系aesthetics · a genealogy of categories

物哀（大西）→幽玄（世阿弥）→粹（九鬼）→侘寂（大西）→阴翳（谷崎）→摩灭（四方田）。六站各有一个可命名的范畴，也各有一位下定义的人。
Aware (Onishi), yugen (Zeami), iki (Kuki), wabi-sabi (Onishi), shadow (Tanizaki), masshitsu (Yomota). Six stations, each with a nameable category and a definer.

丙 · 文学 · 从无常到空无literature · from impermanence to emptiness

徒然草（中世）→荷风（江户挽歌）→漱石（非人情）→芥川（箴语）→川端（空无）。五站之间是六百年的语气变化，不是进步。
Tsurezuregusa, Kafu, Soseki, Akutagawa, Kawabata: six centuries of changing voice, not of progress.

阅读路径 · 三步Reading order · three steps

入门 · 先建立问题Entry · build the question

一本问「我们为何读日本」，一本给美学的概念底座，一本示范如何把日子写成文章。三本读完，全批的坐标就有了。
One asks why we read Japan, one supplies the aesthetic base, one shows how to write a day into an essay. After three, the batch has coordinates.

深入 · 进入范畴Deeper · enter the categories

粹、侘寂、阴翳、摩灭：四个范畴，四种看世界的方式。读时不要急着统合，先让它们互相冲突。
Iki, wabi-sabi, shadow, masshitsu: four categories, four ways of seeing. Do not harmonise them too soon; let them conflict.

贯通 · 两条长线Through · two long lines

先走「甲」线（黄→戴→菊→加藤），再走「丙」线（荷风→芥川→川端）。一条问日本是什么，一条问美如何消逝。
Take the intellectual line first (Huang, Dai, Benedict, Kato), then the literary line (Kafu, Akutagawa, Kawabata). One asks what Japan is, the other how beauty passes.
