背景 · Background背景 · Background

Evans（剑桥大学 Regius 历史讲座教授 2008–2014 · FBA · 2012 年封爵 · Gresham College 校长）第七卷 1815–1914。时间一百年、地理全欧洲。核心论题：这是一个「权力被追求」的世纪——民族主义、自由主义、社会主义、帝国主义四大意识形态互相竞争；工业化重塑社会结构；科学（进化论、细菌学说、物理学）改变认知框架；1914 年一切走向灾难。2016 年出版即获广泛好评，被誉为「企鹅欧洲史系列中最出色的一卷」。
By Evans (Regius Professor of History Cambridge 2008–2014, FBA, knighted 2012, Provost of Gresham College since 2014; author of the Third Reich Trilogy). Volume 7, 1815–1914. One century, all of Europe. Core thesis: a century of 'power pursued' — nationalism, liberalism, socialism, imperialism competing; industrialization reshaping society; science (evolution, germ theory, physics) altering cognitive frameworks; 1914 heading to catastrophe. Hailed on publication as 'the strongest volume in the Penguin Europe series'.

评价与合成 · Reception & synthesis评价与合成 · Reception & synthesis

「当代英语世界最权威的19世纪欧洲史」——The Guardian、TLS 一致好评。 体量与视角是主要保留：近千页、材料密度极高，对普通读者门槛不低；东欧、巴尔干与殖民帝国虽优于多数同类通史，主轴仍是「革命—民族国家—工业化」的西北欧轨迹。埃文斯以大量转引二手文献支撑全景，个别领域（全球经济、生态、技术）依赖他人研究而非原创综合，被一些评论者认为「最好的部分是叙述、最弱的是判断」；性别与种族线索虽前所未有地被织入，却有时呈专题拼贴而非贯穿的分析轴。 **合成 Synthesis：** 「权力的追求」既是民族国家间的权力政治，也是阶级、性别、种族的内部权力争夺——Evans 把19世纪写成全方位权力竞争的世纪。
'The most authoritative 19th-century European history in contemporary English' — unanimous Guardian, TLS acclaim. Scale and vantage are the reservations: nearly a thousand pages of very high data density daunt general readers; and though Eastern Europe, the Balkans and the colonial empires fare better than most syntheses, the spine remains a north-west European 'revolution-nation-state-industrialisation' trajectory. Evans sustains the panorama with heavy reliance on secondary literature, so some reviewers find the best parts narrative and the weakest judgement, notably in global economy, ecology and technology; gender and race, though unprecedentedly woven in, can read as topical insertions rather than a through-going analytic axis. **Synthesis:** 'Power pursued' is both inter-state power politics AND internal struggles over class, gender, race — Evans writes the 19th century as a totality of power competition.

通读札记 · Read-through notesRead-through notes

通读结论Overall verdict

Evans 全书 8 章写完 1815-1914 的『权力的世纪』。通读之后可以看清：本书与 Hobsbawm『革命的世纪』三部曲的根本分歧不在时间划分而在主线 —— Hobsbawm 的主线是资本主义生产方式，Evans 的主线是权力的多维竞争（民族主义、自由主义、社会主义、帝国主义四套语言互相争夺合法性）。全书结构上是前 4 章（政治-社会-经济-阶级）+ 后 4 章（知识-情感-民主-帝国）的双层布局。第 5-6 章（征服自然 / 情感的时代）是本书最独特的贡献 —— 一部欧洲 19 世纪的科学-感官双史。全书第 8 章『Empire』通向 1914 的方式是最值得注意的 —— Evans 论证：到 1914 年主要欧洲国家都在自我说服一场谁也不想要的大战，这是帝国作为集体心理的最终证明。
Evans's 8 chapters describe the century of power 1815-1914. On a full read the difference from Hobsbawm's Age of Revolution is not periodisation but the main line: Hobsbawm's is capitalist mode of production; Evans's is multi-dimensional competition of power — nationalism, liberalism, socialism, imperialism as four languages competing for legitimacy. Structurally: Chs. 1-4 (politics, society, economy, class) + Chs. 5-8 (knowledge, emotion, democracy, empire). Chs. 5-6 (Conquest of Nature / Age of Emotion) are his most original contribution — a twin history of 19th-c. European science and senses. Ch. 8's route to 1914 is the sharpest: by 1914 every major European state had talked itself into a war nobody actually wanted — the final proof of empire as collective psychology.

六国同叙事是本书的方法内核Six-country simultaneity is the core method

Evans 拒绝国别分章 —— 每一章都同时处理英法德俄奥意六国，让 1848 / 1861 / 1870 / 1905 等节点在六国语境中互相显形。这一方法在英语通史中罕见，也是与 Hobsbawm 单线叙事最直接的区别。
Evans rejects country-per-chapter; every chapter runs UK-France-Germany-Russia-Austria-Italy simultaneously, so 1848/1861/1870/1905 appear mutually refracted. Rare in English general history; the sharpest contrast to Hobsbawm.

第 5 章『征服自然』的双重意义Ch.5 Conquest of Nature's double meaning

表面上是农业-工业技术史；实际论点是 —— 1850-1880 的欧洲同时征服了外部自然（化肥、铁路、电报）与内部自然（细菌学、麻醉、统计治理）。这一双重命题让 Ch.5 与 Ch.6 情感形成镜像。
Surface: agrarian-industrial tech history. Real thesis: 1850-1880 Europe simultaneously conquered external nature (fertiliser, rail, telegraph) and internal nature (bacteriology, anaesthesia, statistical governance) — mirroring Ch.6 on emotion.

第 6 章『情感的时代』的文化史野心Ch.6 Age of Emotion as cultural ambition

Evans 把小说、音乐、绘画、宗教复兴、性科学全部处理为『情感成为知识对象』的同一进程 —— 从简·奥斯汀到威尔第到弗洛伊德的一条线。这是全书最像新文化史的一章，也是最不 19 世纪史家通常处理的一章。
Novel, music, painting, evangelical revival, sex science — all treated as one process: emotion becoming an object of knowledge, from Austen to Verdi to Freud. The book's most New-Cultural-History-flavoured chapter.

第 8 章通向 1914 的宿命感Ch.8's fatalism toward 1914

Evans 用『新帝国主义不是力量的过度而是焦虑的外溢』这一反转命题收尾 —— 通读全书之前的政治-社会-文化-科学各章，全部指向这一句：1914 是欧洲作为整体的自我说服。这个收尾把全书从通史变成了论证。
'New imperialism not overreach of strength but leakage of anxiety' — the whole book's politics, society, culture, science converge into: 1914 is Europe's collective self-persuasion. This turns the survey into argument.

全书精读评析 · Full-read critical analysisFull-read critical analysis

通读后的整体判断：Evans 本书是英语世界 19 世纪欧洲通史最完整的一次重写 —— 与直接对话对象 Hobsbawm 三部曲相比：Hobsbawm 用生产方式作为主线，Evans 用权力的多维竞争作为主线；Hobsbawm 的叙述偏向英法德，Evans 严格执行六国 (UK-France-Germany-Russia-Austria-Hungary-Italy) 同叙事；Hobsbawm 的 19 世纪是『长 19 世纪』(1789-1914)，Evans 的 19 世纪从 1815 起。全书最原创的贡献是 Ch.5-6 的科学-情感双联画 —— 这是任何 19 世纪欧洲通史里都还没有过的处理。局限：(1) 对东欧 (俄罗斯帝国境内非俄罗斯民族、奥匈内的捷克-克罗地亚-罗马尼亚) 的处理以帝国为单位而非以民族为单位 —— 民族主义的复杂性被压缩；(2) 对欧洲之外的关系 (美国、日本、中国、拉美) 只在 Ch.3 与 Ch.8 触及 —— 与同期『全球 19 世纪』(Bayly, Beckert) 相比是刻意的收窄；(3) 全书插图密度大 —— 约每 15 页一幅 —— 但图的引用与文本论证的关系并不总是一致，有些图属于装饰性插图。
**Overall verdict after a full read**: Evans' is the most complete English-language rewrite of 19th-c. European general history. Against its explicit interlocutor Hobsbawm: Hobsbawm's main line is mode of production, Evans' is multi-dimensional competition of power; Hobsbawm leans UK-France-Germany, Evans strictly runs UK-France-Germany-Russia-Austria-Hungary-Italy simultaneously; Hobsbawm's long 19th c. runs 1789-1914, Evans' 1815-1914. The most original contribution is Chs.5-6's science-emotion diptych — no other 19th-c. European survey does this. Limitations: (1) Eastern Europe (non-Russian peoples inside the Empire, Czech-Croatian-Romanian inside Austria-Hungary) treated by empire rather than nationality — nationalist complexity is compressed; (2) extra-European relations (US, Japan, China, Latin America) only touched in Chs.3 and 8 — deliberately narrower than contemporary 'global 19th century' (Bayly, Beckert); (3) high illustration density (~1 per 15 pp) but the image-text argument isn't always aligned — some plates are decorative.

Some, indeed, have decided that the most meaningful period to study is the Age of Revolution , to quote the title of the first volume of Eric Hobsbawm’s survey of the years 1789 to 1914, published in 1962. Hobsbawm’s periodization was followed by Jonathan Sperber in his Revolutionary Europe (2000), covering the years 1789–1848, the same period as Hobsbawm’s first volume. Yet there is a price to pay for choosing these years, for what came after was a very different Europe, one much less easy to conceptualize in a single framework. Not by chance, Sperber’s follow-up volume has a wordy title that conveys, no doubt unconsciously, the difficulty he encountered in finding a unifying theme: Europe 1850–1914: Progress, Participation and Apprehension (2008). Hobsbawm went on to write two more volumes, The Age of Capital (1975) covering the years 1848 to 1875, and The Age of Empire (1987), taking the story up to the First World War. Anyone who attempts to write a history of nineteenth-century Europe has to confront these three magnificent books, which tower over the literature on the period. And, with his uncanny gift for conceptual innovation, Hobsbawm went on to characterize the whole period covered by his trilogy as ‘the long nineteenth century’, a model followed by many textbooks and primers, for example William Simpson and Martin Jones’s Europe 1783–1914 (2000). The long nineteenth century is, however, a broken-backed century, divided into two very different halves by the 1848 Revolutions. Not surprisingly, like Sperber, many historians covering the period from the French Revolution or the defeat of Napoleon to the First World War have given up the attempt to find any kind of conceptual unity, and have chosen anodyne titles such as Europe’s Uncertain Path (2012), to quote R. S. Alexander’s recent political narrative.EN · english

Preface — original text
Through most of the twentieth century, historians regarded the rise of nation states and the conflicts between them as the central features of European history in the nineteenth century. The triumph of nationalism forged new political and cultural entities and inspired revolts against large and, it seemed, outmoded multinational empires, revolts against oppression by other nationalities or ambitions to achieve dominance over them. This model of the nation state was exported across the globe in the twentieth century, making its emergence in Europe in the nineteenth seem even more important. Historians once saw this process in positive terms, putting celebratory accounts of the unification of Italy and Germany, the growth of Czech and Polish national consciousness, and other products of the age of nationalism at the centre of their narrative. As national and ethnic rivalries spilled over into the vast conflagration of the Second World War, however, the rise of nationalism appeared in a darker light, a view underlined by the Balkan wars of the 1990s. But since then we have come increasingly to live in an age of globalization, as the barriers created by the Cold War have crumbled, international institutions, worldwide means of communication, multinational companies, and many other influences have eroded national boundaries and begun to bind us all together as a global human community. Since the turn of the century, this has also altered our vision of the past, which historians have come to see increasingly in a global perspective. The call for the writing of global history is not in itself new: it was issued as long ago as the 1970s by the French historian Marc Ferro and was present in the concept of Universalgeschichte (universal history) as practised by Leopold von Ranke in the nineteenth century or Arnold Toynbee and William H. McNeill in the twentieth. But a global history that links the different parts of the world rather than telling their discrete stories has emerged only recently, as historians have begun to examine subjects such as the effects of empire on European economies, societies, cultures and political systems, notably but not exclusively those of Britain; the global economic ties that bound Europe to other parts of the world in a nexus of mutual interaction; and the rise of worldwide empire as a common European process rather than one specific to any particular European nation. Historians have also been busy rewriting the history of individual European nations in a global context, emphasizing the effects of European diasporas – the millions of Europeans who emigrated to other parts of the globe – on the ‘mother country’, the infusion of European nationalism with elements of racial theory derived from the experience of colonization in Africa or Asia, and the emergence of global geopolitics as a key factor in relations between European states.EN · english
