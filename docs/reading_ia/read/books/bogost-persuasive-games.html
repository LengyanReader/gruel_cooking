背景Background

Bogost 以「程序修辞」分析政治、广告与教育游戏，主张游戏有一种独立的表达形式。 《Persuasive Games》2007 年由 MIT Press 出版，是「程序修辞」的核心文本。Bogost 主张游戏的说服力不在文字或图像，而在规则与流程：玩家在操作规则时，也在一套论证里行动。 它也可以被读成一次「媒介声明」：游戏不是「互动版文章」，而是一种以规则为媒介的论证形式。Bogost 把「程序修辞」放在文字、图像之外，给游戏一个独立的批评位置。
Bogost uses procedural rhetoric to analyse political, advertising and educational games, arguing that games have a distinct expressive form. Persuasive Games (MIT Press, 2007) is the core text of procedural rhetoric. Bogost argues that a game persuades not through words or images but through rules and processes: in operating rules, players also move inside an argument. It can also be read as a media claim: games are not 'interactive articles' but a form of argument mediated by rules. Bogost places procedural rhetoric beside words and images, giving games an independent critical position.

长评 · Critical essayCritical essay

规则如何说服：读《说服性游戏》How rules persuade

程序修辞：游戏的说服力不在文字，而在规则与流程。Procedural rhetoric: a game persuades through rules and processes, not words.

《Persuasive Games》提出「程序修辞」：游戏通过规则与流程说服，而不是通过文字。它把政治、广告与教育游戏放在一起，主张游戏有一种独立的表达形式。

它的力量在于给游戏研究一个可分析的单位：规则如何论证；它的风险也在这里：玩家未必把规则读成论证，作者也可能高估设计意图。

读它时最好把「程序修辞」当作一个假设：这个游戏的规则在论证什么？玩家是否真的经历了这个论证？如果答案是否定的，程序修辞就只是分析者的读法，而不是游戏的效果。

最强的反读是：玩家可能只经历规则，而不把规则读成论证；程序修辞或许是分析者的建构。若如此，本书应被读成「如何分析游戏」，而不是「游戏如何说服」。

若把程序修辞当作「游戏一定在说服」，会高估设计意图；它更像一把分析工具：先问规则在论证什么，再问玩家是否经历这个论证。

英文摘要 · AbstractAbstract

Persuasive Games proposes procedural rhetoric: games persuade through rules and processes rather than words. It places political, advertising and educational games together, arguing that games have a distinct expressive form.

Its power is to give game studies an analysable unit: how rules argue; its risk is that players may not read rules as argument and the author may overestimate intent.

Read procedural rhetoric as a hypothesis: what does this game's rule system argue, and do players actually undergo that argument? If not, procedural rhetoric is the analyst's reading rather than the game's effect.

The strongest counter-reading: players may undergo rules without reading them as argument; procedural rhetoric may be the analyst's construction. On that reading the book is about how to analyse games, not how games persuade.

Read as 'games always persuade', procedural rhetoric overestimates intent; it is better as an analytic tool: what do the rules argue, then do players undergo it?
