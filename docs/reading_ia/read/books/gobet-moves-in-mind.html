长评 · Critical essayCritical essay

不止国际象棋：读《心中的棋步》Beyond chess

把棋类心理学扩展到围棋、将棋、跳棋：不同棋盘游戏共享哪些认知机制？Extends chess psychology to Go, shogi and checkers: which cognitive mechanisms do board games share?

《Moves in Mind》把「棋盘游戏」作为比较单位，而不是只看国际象棋。它先讲国际象棋的证据，再扩展到围棋、将棋等，最后讨论跨游戏的共同机制。

它的力量在于打破「国际象棋中心主义」；它的风险也在这里：多游戏比较可能牺牲单游戏深度，文化差异也可能被认知框架吸收。

读它时最好把「共享机制」当作可检验的假设：哪些结论能跨游戏迁移，哪些不能？如果围棋的「势」、将棋的「持驹」都无法被同一模型吸收，那「棋盘游戏心理学」就还需要更多的文化维度。

最强的反读是：所谓「跨游戏」可能只是把国际象棋的组块结论翻译到其他棋类；真正的差异（围棋的势、将棋的持驹）仍未被模型吸收。若如此，本书是「比较的邀请」，而不是「比较的完成」。

若把跨游戏比较当作「把国际象棋结论翻译过去」，会漏掉差异：围棋的势、将棋的持驹，可能不是同一组块模型能吸收的。

英文摘要 · AbstractAbstract

Moves in Mind takes board games as the comparative unit rather than chess alone. It starts with chess evidence, extends to Go, shogi and others, and closes on shared mechanisms.

Its power is to break chess-centrism; its risk is that multi-game comparison may sacrifice depth and absorb cultural difference into a cognitive frame.

Read shared mechanisms as a testable hypothesis: which conclusions transfer across games, and which do not? If Go's 'influence' or shogi's 'drops' cannot be absorbed by the same model, board-game psychology still needs more cultural dimensions.

The strongest counter-reading: the cross-game move may merely translate chess chunking into other games; real differences — Go's influence, shogi's drops — remain unabsorbed. On that reading the book is an invitation to compare, not the completion of comparison.

Read cross-game comparison as translating chess conclusions and difference is lost: Go's influence, shogi's drops may not fit one chunk model.
