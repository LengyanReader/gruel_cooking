背景Background

本书基于 Escobar 在哥伦比亚太平洋沿岸地区多年的合作研究，对象是非洲裔社区（尤其 proceso de comunidades negras 运动）与他们的领地组织。这一地区在 1990 年代获得集体土地权（Ley 70），同时被卷入生物多样性热、棕榈油种植、伐木、矿业与准军事暴力。Escobar 与地方组织、NGO 与研究者合作，追踪「领地」如何从一个法律范畴变成一种生命世界的政治：河流不是资源，而是道路与亲属关系；森林不是碳汇，而是历史与知识的载体。书名四个词——地方、运动、生活、网络——就是全书的结构。
The book grows out of years of collaborative research on Colombia's Pacific coast with Afro-Colombian communities, especially the proceso de comunidades negras movement and its territorial organisations. In the 1990s the region won collective land rights (Ley 70) while being pulled into biodiversity booms, oil-palm plantations, logging, mining and paramilitary violence. Working with local organisations, NGOs and researchers, Escobar traces how 'territory' turns from a legal category into a politics of lifeworld: the river is not a resource but a road and a kinship relation; the forest is not a carbon sink but a carrier of history and knowledge. The four words of the subtitle — place, movements, life, redes — are the book's structure.

结构Structure

全书分四部分，层层放大：先写「地方」——太平洋沿岸的地理、河流与村落如何构成一个关系性的生活空间；再写「资本」——生物多样性、棕榈油、林业与「发展」如何把地方重新编码为资源；第三部分写「生活」——社区如何用领地、食物、仪式与教育维持生命世界；最后写「网络」——地方组织如何与跨国倡议、学术网络与全球环境治理连接。全书不是线性叙事，而是四个尺度的叠合，每一部分都回指其他部分。
Four parts scale outward: place — how the geography, rivers and villages of the Pacific coast form a relational living space; capital — how biodiversity, oil palm, forestry and 'development' recode place as resource; life — how communities sustain a lifeworld through territory, food, ritual and education; and redes — how local organisations connect to transnational advocacy, scholarly networks and global environmental governance. It is not a linear narrative but four scales superimposed, each pointing back to the others.

核心论题 · ThesisThesis

评价与合成Reception & synthesis

出版后成为政治生态学、领地研究与拉丁美洲研究的核心文本；「领地作为生命世界」「redes」被广泛引用，也常与 Porto-Gonçalves、Haesbaert 的领地理论并读。 批评集中在两点：一是与社区组织的合作关系可能影响批判距离；二是「领地」概念在跨区域使用时容易被理想化，掩盖社区内部的分歧与性别不平等。 **合成 Synthesis：** 本书的关键贡献是把「替代」从抽象主张变成可追踪的实践：领地不是被保护的资源，而是被集体生产与维护的生命世界。
It became a core text in political ecology, territory studies and Latin American studies; territory as lifeworld and redes are widely cited and read with Porto-Gonçalves and Haesbaert on territory. Criticism centres on two points: collaborative relations with community organisations may affect critical distance, and the concept of territory can be idealised when used across regions, obscuring internal divisions and gender inequality. **Synthesis:** The key contribution is to turn 'alternatives' from an abstract claim into a traceable practice: territory is not a resource to be protected but a lifeworld collectively produced and maintained.

推断 · The claimThe claim

Escobar 要证明：替代性政治不是理论构想，而是在具体领地中被集体生产的生命世界；因此研究者应追踪地方运动如何把法律、生态与历史转译成「另一个世界」的实践。

Escobar wants to show that alternative politics is not a theoretical construct but a lifeworld collectively produced in concrete territories; research should therefore trace how local movements translate law, ecology and history into the practice of 'another world'.

长评 · Critical essayCritical essay

领地作为生命世界：读《差异的领地》Territory as lifeworld

替代不是抽象主张，而是被集体生产与维护的生命世界——河流是道路，森林是历史。Alternatives are not abstract claims but lifeworlds collectively produced and maintained — the river is a road, the forest is history.

《差异的领地》是《遭遇发展》之后必须读的一本书，因为它回答了前者留下的问题：拆掉发展话语之后，还剩什么？Escobar 的答案是「领地」——但不是土地所有权意义上的领地，而是哥伦比亚太平洋沿岸黑人社区用河流、森林、食物、仪式与亲属关系共同维护的生命世界。

这本书的结构值得学习：place、movements、life、redes 四个词既是章节，也是四个尺度。地方不是全球的对立面，而是全球力量被具体感受的地方；运动不是抽象政治，而是把法律范畴转成文化实践的过程；生活不是背景，而是政治的内容；网络不是逃离地方，而是让地方连接到跨国倡议的方式。

它的力量来自合作研究的深度：Escobar 不假装站在外部观察，而是明确自己与社区组织、NGO 的关系。这种立场也让批评成为可能——读者可以问，当研究者与运动站在一起时，他还能看见社区内部的性别与阶级分歧吗？

这本书最适合与 Tsing 的《钻石女王的领地》对读：一个从国家边缘的黑人社区出发，一个从印尼的梅拉瑙人出发；两人都在问，被国家视为「偏远」的地方，如何可能是世界生成的现场。

英文摘要 · AbstractAbstract

Territories of Difference is the necessary sequel to Encountering Development because it answers the question the earlier book left open: what remains after development discourse is dismantled? Escobar's answer is territory — not land ownership but a lifeworld maintained by Afro-Colombian communities on the Pacific coast through rivers, forests, food, ritual and kinship.

The structure is worth learning from: place, movements, life and redes are both chapters and scales. Place is not the opposite of the global but where global forces are concretely felt; movements are not abstract politics but the conversion of legal categories into cultural practice; life is not background but the content of politics; networks are not escape from place but the way place connects to transnational advocacy.

Its force comes from the depth of collaboration: Escobar does not pretend to observe from outside but marks his relations with community organisations and NGOs. That stance also enables criticism — one can ask whether a researcher standing with a movement can still see its internal gender and class divisions.

The book is best read against Tsing's In the Realm of the Diamond Queen: one starts from an Afro-Colombian community at the state's edge, the other from the Meratus in Indonesia; both ask how a place the state treats as remote can be a site where worlds are made.
