背景Background

《关联性》由 Escobar、Michal Osterweil 与 Kriti Sharma 合著，2022 年由 Bloomsbury 出版。三人分别来自人类学/后发展、社会运动理论与科学哲学，全书试图把「关系性」从一个哲学立场变成一种政治纲领：如果一切存在都是关系性的，那么政治就不只是分配资源，而是维护、切断与重组关系。书的结构围绕几个问题展开：关系本体论的谱系、超越人类的关系、涌现政治（emergent politics）、以及关系性如何指导实践。
Relationality is co-authored by Escobar, Michal Osterweil and Kriti Sharma and published by Bloomsbury in 2022. The three come from anthropology/post-development, social movement theory and philosophy of science, and the book turns relationality from a philosophical position into a political programme: if all being is relational, politics is not only distributing resources but sustaining, severing and recomposing relations. Chapters circle several questions: the genealogy of relational ontologies, relations beyond the human, emergent politics, and how relationality guides practice.

论证结构 · ArgumentArgument

结构Structure

全书以理论章与案例章交替推进。理论部分梳理关系本体论的不同来源：过程哲学、本体论人类学、女性主义科学研究与原住民宇宙论；案例部分讨论气候行动、食物主权、社区经济与多物种实践。结论提出「涌现政治」：不是先有主体再有关系，而是关系先于主体，政治因此是关系的事件。
Theoretical and case chapters alternate. Theory traces the sources of relational ontology: process philosophy, ontological anthropology, feminist science studies and indigenous cosmologies; cases discuss climate action, food sovereignty, community economies and multispecies practice. The conclusion proposes 'emergent politics': subjects do not precede relations; relations precede subjects, so politics is an event of relation.

评价与合成Reception & synthesis

出版后进入关系社会学、环境人文与后发展讨论；它被视为 Escobar 从「多元宇宙」走向「关系本体论」的总结性文本。 作为合著，三人的声音不完全统一；「关系性」被批评为过于宽泛，几乎可以解释一切，因而难以证伪。 **合成 Synthesis：** 本书的贡献是把「关系」从描述词变成政治判准：一个实践是否解放，取决于它维护了哪些关系、切断了哪些关系。
It entered relational sociology, environmental humanities and post-development debate and is read as Escobar's summing-up move from the pluriverse to relational ontology. As a co-authored work the three voices do not fully harmonise, and relationality is criticised as so broad that it can explain anything and is hard to falsify. **Synthesis:** The contribution is to turn relation from a descriptor into a political criterion: whether a practice is emancipatory depends on which relations it sustains and which it severs.

推断 · The claimThe claim

三位作者要证明：存在是关系性的，因此政治不是主体之间的资源分配，而是关系的维护、切断与重组；这一本体论转向要求重新定义自由、自主与共同体。

The authors want to show that being is relational, so politics is not resource distribution among subjects but the sustaining, severing and recomposing of relations; this ontological turn requires redefining freedom, autonomy and community.

长评 · Critical essayCritical essay

关系先于主体：读《关联性》Relations before subjects

如果存在是关系性的，那么政治就不是分配资源，而是维护、切断与重组关系。If being is relational, politics is not distributing resources but sustaining, severing and recomposing relations.

《关联性》是 Escobar 与 Osterweil、Sharma 的合著，也是他思想的一次收束：从《遭遇发展》的话语批判，到《差异的领地》的领地政治，再到《多元宇宙的设计》的本体论设计，最后落到「关系」这个最基础的概念。

书的核心命题是：存在不是孤立的实体，而是关系的事件。这个命题听起来像哲学，却有直接的政治后果。如果主体不是先于关系而存在，那么政治就不能从「谁的利益」开始，而要从「哪些关系被维护、哪些被切断」开始。自由因此不是摆脱关系，而是在关系中获得自决；共同体不是成员的集合，而是在关系中被不断重做的共同生活。

这本书的优点是跨学科：三位作者分别带来人类学、社会运动理论与科学哲学的视角，案例覆盖气候、食物与多物种。缺点也来自这里：三人的声音不完全统一，「关系性」宽泛到几乎可以解释一切，因而难以证伪；对关系中的支配与暴力，分析仍显不足。

读它最好的方式，是把它当作一组问题而不是一套答案：你正在维护哪些关系？切断了哪些？这些关系的重组，让谁更有能力生活，又让谁更难？

英文摘要 · AbstractAbstract

Relationality is Escobar's co-authored book with Osterweil and Sharma and a gathering of his thought: from the discourse critique of Encountering Development, through the territorial politics of Territories of Difference and the ontological design of Designs for the Pluriverse, to the most basic concept of all, relation.

The core proposition is that being is not isolated substance but an event of relation. It sounds philosophical but has direct political consequences. If subjects do not precede relations, politics cannot start from 'whose interests' but from 'which relations are sustained and which severed'. Freedom is therefore not escape from relation but self-determination within it; community is not a set of members but shared life continually remade in relation.

The strength is interdisciplinarity: three authors bring anthropology, social movement theory and philosophy of science, with cases from climate, food and multispecies life. The weakness comes from the same place: the voices do not fully harmonise, relationality is broad enough to explain almost anything and is hard to falsify, and the analysis of domination and violence within relations remains thin.

The best way to read it is as a set of questions rather than answers: which relations are you sustaining, which severing, and who becomes more able to live — or less — as they are recomposed?
