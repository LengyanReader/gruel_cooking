背景Background

de Groot 1946 年以荷兰文出版，1965 年英译；其「出声思维」方法后来成为专家研究的标准工具。 《Thought and Choice in Chess》1946 年以荷兰文出版，1965 年英译（Mouton）。de Groot 用出声思维记录棋手如何选择着法，发现高手不是穷举，而是「一眼看出」局面的结构。 它也可以被读成一次「方法示范」：在专家研究还没有标准工具时，de Groot 用出声思维把「思考」变成可记录的语料。后来的组块研究、AI 专家系统都受益于这次示范。
Published in Dutch in 1946 and translated in 1965; its think-aloud method became a standard tool for expertise research. Thought and Choice in Chess appeared in Dutch in 1946 and in English in 1965 (Mouton). de Groot used think-aloud protocols to record move choice and found that masters do not enumerate but see a position's structure at a glance. It can also be read as a methodological demonstration: before expertise research had standard tools, de Groot used think-aloud to turn thought into recordable data. Later chunking research and AI expert systems benefited from the demonstration.

长评 · Critical essayCritical essay

一眼看出：读《思维与选择》Seeing at a glance

de Groot 的开山之作：用出声思维研究棋手如何选择着法，发现高手「一眼看出」而非穷举。de Groot's founding work: think-aloud studies of move choice show masters see at a glance rather than enumerate.

《Thought and Choice in Chess》是棋类心理学的开山之作。de Groot 用出声思维记录棋手如何选择着法，发现高手不是穷举，而是「一眼看出」局面的结构。

它的力量在于把「思维」变成可记录的语料；它的风险也在这里：出声思维可能改变思考，实验室任务也与真实对局有差距。

读它时最好把「一眼识别」当作一个可检验的假设：如果高手真是识别，那么训练就应该改变「看」的方式，而不只是增加「想」的时间。这也是 Gobet 后来用「组块」继续追问的地方。

最强的反读是：出声思维记录的是「棋手说自己怎么想」，而不是「棋手实际怎么想」；实验室任务也与真实对局有差距。若如此，本书的证据应被读成「报告」，而不是「思维本身」。

若把口述当作「思维本身」，会忽略报告与思维之间的缝隙；de Groot 的价值，是让我们看见这条缝隙，并把「一眼识别」变成可检验的假设。

英文摘要 · AbstractAbstract

Thought and Choice in Chess is the founding work of chess psychology. de Groot used think-aloud protocols to record move choice and found that masters do not enumerate but see a position's structure at a glance.

Its power is to turn thought into recordable data; its risk is that think-aloud may alter thinking and lab tasks differ from real games.

Read 'seeing at a glance' as a testable hypothesis: if experts recognise, training should change how they see, not only how long they think. This is where Gobet's chunks later continue the inquiry.

The strongest counter-reading: think-aloud records what players say they think, not what they actually think; lab tasks also differ from real games. On that reading the evidence is a report, not thought itself.

Treat verbal report as thought itself and the gap between report and thought disappears; de Groot's value is to show the gap and turn the glance into a testable hypothesis.
