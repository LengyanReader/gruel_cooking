背景Background

这是 Tsing 在《世界末日的菌菇》之前发表的一篇关键论文，刊于《Environmental Humanities》创刊号（2012）。它把「伴生物种」（companion species，借自 Haraway）用于菌菇：菌菇既不是被驯化的作物，也不是纯粹野生的自然，而是在人类扰动过的边缘地带生长的「不羁」生命。Tsing 用松茸、香菇与真菌的案例，提出多物种研究不应从「物种」开始，而应从「相遇」开始。
This is a key essay published before The Mushroom at the End of the World, in the first volume of Environmental Humanities (2012). It applies 'companion species' (borrowed from Haraway) to fungi: mushrooms are neither domesticated crops nor pure wild nature but 'unruly' life growing on edges disturbed by humans. Through matsutake, shiitake and fungal cases, Tsing argues that multispecies research should start not from species but from encounters.

结构Structure

文章以短论形式推进：先质疑「自然/文化」与「野生/驯化」的二分，再以菌菇的生命史说明「不羁的边缘」如何被人类活动制造，最后把「伴生物种」提升为一种方法论——研究共同生成，而不是研究孤立的物。
The essay proceeds as a short argument: it first questions the nature/culture and wild/domesticated binaries, then uses fungal life histories to show how unruly edges are produced by human activity, and finally elevates companion species into a method — study co-becoming rather than isolated objects.

核心论题 · ThesisThesis

评价与合成Reception & synthesis

这篇论文后来被视为 Tsing 多物种转向的方法宣言，常与 Haraway 的「伴生物种」和《菌菇》一起引用。 作为短篇，它无法展开完整的田野论证；「不羁」也容易被读成一种浪漫化的野生性。 **合成 Synthesis：** 它的价值在于提出问题而非给出答案：如果物种总是在相遇中生成，那么「自然」与「文化」的分类本身就值得重写。
The essay is now treated as a methodological manifesto for Tsing's multispecies turn and is often cited with Haraway's companion species and Mushroom. As a short essay it cannot develop a full field argument, and 'unruly' can be read as a romanticised wildness. **Synthesis:** Its value is to pose a question rather than answer it: if species always emerge in encounters, the nature/culture distinction itself must be rewritten.

推断 · The claimThe claim

Tsing 要证明：菌菇不是自然/文化二分的对象，而是人类与真菌在边缘共同生成的「伴生物种」；因此多物种研究的起点应是相遇，而非分类。

Tsing wants to show that mushrooms are not objects of a nature/culture divide but companion species co-produced by humans and fungi at edges; multispecies research should therefore start from encounters, not classification.

为什么这样读 · The groundsThe grounds

标题把「不羁」与「边缘」并置，直接挑战「野生/驯化」的边界；文章选择菌菇，是因为它们既在森林中「野生」，又在人类扰动过的土地上生长；「伴生物种」借自 Haraway，但 Tsing 更强调边缘的生态物质性。

The title pairs unruliness with edges, directly challenging the wild/domesticated boundary; fungi are chosen because they are both wild in forests and grow on human-disturbed land; companion species comes from Haraway, but Tsing stresses the ecological materiality of edges.

2012 年刊出后，先在环境人文与多物种研究圈内被引用，随后随着《菌菇》的流行成为 Tsing 方法转向的常用参照。

长评 · Critical essayCritical essay

在边缘与菌丝之间：读《不羁的边缘》Between edges and mycelium

一篇短论文，提前写下了《菌菇》的方法：从相遇开始，而不是从物种开始。A short essay that anticipates Mushroom's method: start from encounters, not species.

《不羁的边缘》不是一本完整的书，而是一篇方法宣言。它的核心动作很小，却很重要：把菌菇从「野生/驯化」的分类里拿出来，放回人类扰动过的边缘。菌菇既不在纯粹的自然里，也不在完全的掌控中；它们在两者之间生长，并把两者都改写。

Tsing 借用 Haraway 的「伴生物种」，但她更关心生态物质性：菌丝如何穿过土壤、树木与人的活动，如何在被砍伐、被耕种、被遗弃的地方继续生长。她由此提出，多物种研究不应从「物种」这个单位开始，而应从「相遇」这个关系开始。

这篇论文的力量在于它的开放性；局限也来自同一处。它没有完整的田野证据，也没有处理「不羁」被浪漫化的风险。它更像一个问题的提出，而不是一个结论的证明。

把它当作《菌菇》的前奏来读，会看见 Tsing 后来所有关键词的雏形：边缘、相遇、共同生成、废墟中的生命。它短，但它打开了一整条路。

英文摘要 · AbstractAbstract

Unruly Edges is not a complete book but a methodological manifesto. Its central move is small but important: it takes mushrooms out of the wild/domesticated classification and returns them to edges disturbed by humans. Mushrooms are neither in pure nature nor under full control; they grow between the two and rewrite both.

Tsing borrows Haraway's companion species but cares more about ecological materiality: how mycelium passes through soil, trees and human activity, and keeps growing in logged, cultivated and abandoned places. She argues that multispecies research should start not from the unit 'species' but from the relation 'encounter'.

The essay's strength is its openness; its limit comes from the same place. It has no full field evidence and does not address the risk of romanticising the unruly. It is closer to posing a question than proving a conclusion.

Read as a prelude to Mushroom, it shows the germ of all Tsing's later keywords: edges, encounters, co-becoming, life in ruins. It is short, but it opens a whole road.
