背景Background

《在受损星球上生活的艺术》由 Tsing 与 Nils Bubandt、Elaine Gan、Heather Anne Swanson 合编，源自一次关于人类世与多物种生活的跨学科会议。全书分「幽灵」（Ghosts）与「怪物」（Monsters）两部分：幽灵指过去暴力与毒性留下的长期遗产，怪物指新出现的、跨物种的混合体。编者要问的不是「如何拯救地球」，而是「在已经受损的星球上，如何学习共同生活」。
Arts of Living on a Damaged Planet is edited by Tsing with Nils Bubandt, Elaine Gan and Heather Anne Swanson, growing out of an interdisciplinary conference on the Anthropocene and multispecies life. It is divided into 'Ghosts' and 'Monsters': ghosts are the long legacies of past violence and toxicity, monsters are emergent cross-species assemblages. The editors ask not how to save the earth but how to learn to live together on an already damaged planet.

论证结构 · ArgumentArgument

结构Structure

这不是一部单作者专著，而是一部编排过的文集：两部分各自收录人类学、生物学、艺术史与环境研究的文章，彼此之间以短评与图像连接。「幽灵」部分从毒性、殖民暴力与物种灭绝的遗留写起；「怪物」部分从杂交、共生与新生态组合写起。两种写法共同拒绝「人类世」作为单一整体，强调其不均匀、可感知、可讲述的局部。
This is not a single-author monograph but an edited collection: each part gathers essays from anthropology, biology, art history and environmental studies, linked by short commentaries and images. 'Ghosts' begins from the legacies of toxicity, colonial violence and extinction; 'Monsters' begins from hybridity, symbiosis and new ecological assemblages. Together they refuse the Anthropocene as a single whole and stress its uneven, perceptible, tellable localities.

核心论题 · ThesisThesis

评价与合成Reception & synthesis

出版后成为人类世人文研究的重要文集，常与《菌菇》并读；「幽灵」与「怪物」成为讨论多物种遗产与新组合的常用词。 作为文集，各章质量与理论密度不均；「幽灵/怪物」的二分也被批评为过于整齐。 **合成 Synthesis：** 本书的贡献是把「人类世」从全球尺度拉回可感知的局部：幽灵与怪物都是需要在具体地点被注意、被讲述、被回应的东西。
It became an important collection in Anthropocene humanities, often read with Mushroom; ghosts and monsters became common terms for multispecies legacies and emergent assemblages. As an edited collection, the chapters vary in quality and theoretical density, and the ghosts/monsters binary has been criticised as too neat. **Synthesis:** The contribution is to pull the Anthropocene from a global scale back to perceptible localities: ghosts and monsters are things that must be noticed, narrated and answered in specific places.

推断 · The claimThe claim

编者要证明：人类世不是一个可以用单一尺度把握的整体，而是由「幽灵」（过去的遗产）与「怪物」（新生的混合）共同构成的斑驳现场；因此需要新的注意与讲述方式。

The editors want to show that the Anthropocene is not a whole graspable at one scale but a patchy field made of ghosts (legacies of the past) and monsters (emergent hybrids); new ways of noticing and telling are therefore needed.

为什么这样读 · The groundsThe grounds

书名把「生活的艺术」放在「受损星球」之前，强调实践先于拯救；两部分的结构让「回望」与「前瞻」并列；跨学科编选说明单一学科无法处理这种多重时间性。

The title puts arts of living before the damaged planet, stressing practice over rescue; the two-part structure places retrospection and anticipation side by side; the interdisciplinary selection shows that no single discipline can handle this multiple temporality.

长评 · Critical essayCritical essay

与幽灵和怪物共处：读《在受损星球上生活的艺术》Living with ghosts and monsters

人类世不是整体，而是由过去的幽灵与新生的怪物共同构成的斑驳现场。The Anthropocene is not a whole but a patchy field of past ghosts and emergent monsters.

《在受损星球上生活的艺术》首先是一部编排之书。它不试图用一篇导论解释人类世，而是把不同学科的文章放进「幽灵」与「怪物」两个房间：前者回望毒性、殖民暴力与灭绝的遗留，后者前瞻杂交、共生与新生态组合。读者在两部分之间来回，逐渐感到「人类世」不是一个时代，而是一组不均匀的伤口与生长。

这种编排有明确的方法论意图：如果人类世是斑驳的，那么单一尺度的解决方案就是错位的。本书因此不提供全球治理方案，只提供局部的注意技艺——如何看见毒性的长期作用，如何讲述一个新物种的出现，如何在受损的地方继续生活。

局限也很明显：文集各章质量不一，「幽灵/怪物」的二分有时过于文学化，可能遮蔽具体的政治经济差异。它更适合被当作一套感知训练，而不是一部人类世总论。

读它最好的方式，是把它与《菌菇》对读：一个从废墟中的生计出发，一个从受损星球的多重时间出发。两者合起来，才是 Tsing 的多物种世界。

英文摘要 · AbstractAbstract

Arts of Living on a Damaged Planet is first an edited book. It does not try to explain the Anthropocene in one introduction; it places essays from different disciplines in two rooms, ghosts and monsters: the first looks back at toxicity, colonial violence and extinction, the second looks forward to hybridity, symbiosis and new ecological assemblages. Moving between them, the reader comes to feel that the Anthropocene is not an era but a set of uneven wounds and growths.

The arrangement has a methodological point: if the Anthropocene is patchy, single-scale solutions are mismatched. The book therefore offers no global governance plan, only local arts of noticing — how to see the long action of toxicity, how to narrate the arrival of a new species, how to keep living in a damaged place.

The limits are clear: the chapters vary in quality, and the ghosts/monsters binary can be too literary, obscuring concrete political-economic differences. It is better used as a training in perception than as a general theory of the Anthropocene.

The best way to read it is alongside Mushroom: one starts from livelihood in ruins, the other from the multiple temporalities of a damaged planet. Together they form Tsing's multispecies world.
