Literature Review

The review as groundwork

This page is the project's reading groundwork: a verified, annotated review of the scholars whose work gives the project its premises about movement, place, material life, heritage and regeneration. It is designed to be a living reference — the basis for ongoing brainstorming, the combing of ideas and viewpoints, and the transition into substantive research. The companion document (materials/LITERATURE_REVIEW.md) carries the full annotated bibliography, keyed to the mapping of each source to the research questions.

A synthesis in six dimensions

The review organises every involved scholar through six dimensions. Premises: things act; walking makes knowledge; heritage works for future generations; change is the baseline. Perspectives: four analytical lenses — walking/movement, materiality/things/ruins, heritage futures, and connectivity/corridors. Ethos: embrace change, befriend decay, respect local livelihoods, answer to future generations. Method: the three-walk method, following one thing, food-tracing, island comparison, digital ethnography, narrative policy analysis. Practice: field cycles in Beijing, Gotland and Southern Öland, with regeneration observation. Persona: each scholar's academic taste, quality, spirit, character, concerns and philosophy — read from their verified works and positions.

How to read the portraits

The portraits below are academic persona sketches [project reading], derived from each scholar's verified works and institutional positions — never from private life or speculation. Each portrait carries six dimensions: taste (what they are drawn to), quality (scholarly strengths), spirit (working stance), character (temperament), concerns (what keeps them up at night), and philosophy (their deeper commitments). Selected works are prioritised by relevance to this project and source-labelled [A] primary / [B] authoritative secondary.

Extended circle

Around the five core scholars sits an extended circle of verified collaborators and co-authors: Þóra Pétursdóttir (Ruin Memories; Theory Adrift with Olsen), Caitlin DeSilvey (Curated Decay; palliative curation), Sharon Macdonald (Memorylands; Heritage Futures), Rodney Harrison (Heritage: Critical Approaches; Heritage Futures editor), Anders Högberg (Cultural Heritage and the Future with Holtorf; 'No future in archaeological heritage management?'), Jo Vergunst (Ways of Walking with Ingold), Gustav Wollentz and Samantha May (co-authors on heritage-futures), the Shanks–Webmoor–Witmore triangle (Archaeology: The Discipline of Things), the Gotland–Rapa Nui team of Poort, Persson-Fischier, Elf Donaldson and Schaub, and Gillette's co-authors Emma Boyd, Elizabeth Bennett and Milena Arias Schreiber.

Verification & open items

Every citation on this page has been web-verified, and sources are labelled [A] primary / [B] authoritative secondary / [C] general / [D] unverified clue. Known corrections and open items include: Gillette's mining-heritage article is 2024 (Journal of Heritage Tourism 19(1)) with E. Boyd — not 2023; the 2026 Sociologia Ruralis herring-quota paper is verified via Wiley DOI 10.1111/soru.70050, but co-authorship with Singleton is not verified [D]; Ingold's Imagining for Real is treated as Routledge 2022 / openedition 2021; the Ruin Memories project (NRC 2009–2013) and Arctic Silk Road engagement are recorded as project-type entries.
