Fieldwork

Cycle 1 — Beijing immersion

7–14 days exploring Shichahai–Yuhe, Tongzhou, Zhangjiawan and the canal gateway environments. Following the canal–Silk Roads lens in the field: what arrives, what leaves, where transfer occurs, what changes, what remains after the route changes.

Cycle 2 — Gotland

7–10 days comparing the Baltic island context — Visby's Hanseatic past, Ridanäs's trading connections, rural and coastal Gotland.

Cycle 3 — Southern Öland

3–5 days at the regeneration laboratory — Gamla Skogsby's Iron Age evidence and the question of when the past becomes capable of generating future life.

Cycle 4 — Linköping

3–5 days on the Kinda–Göta canal landscape — what happens to cultural meaning when an infrastructure loses its original economic function.

Cycle 5 — Return to Beijing

The logic is circular: BEIJING → SURPRISE → SWEDISH COMPARISON → REVISED THEORY → RETURN TO BEIJING → TEST. The goal is a research practice in which the world can force the theory to change.

Field Observations

Grand Canal of Beijing

  • Locks inside the grid 2026-07-09

    The Qingcheng lock gate (upper lock) is preserved within the modern city grid; the canal beneath the street map is lined with re-created heritage. Living heritage here is staged between daily life and display.

  • Porridge at the old grain wharf 2026-07-02

    Zhangjiawan, the historic tribute-grain wharf: the quay keeps its old alignment while the waterside market opens for breakfast; porridge vendors work the morning crowd where barges once tied up.

  • The canal you can walk 2026-06-28

    Tongzhou, dawn: joggers and yangge troupes move along the Grand Canal park while a museum recreates the 1920s towpath. The canal lives twice - as daily amenity and as displayed heritage.

  • Grain arrives by water 2026-06-12

    At Shichahai-Yuhe, morning: the water gates carry the memory of grain tribute barges. What arrives, what leaves, where transfer occurs, what changes.

Gotland

  • Ridanäs is not on the tentative list 2026-09-11

    Desk record, 2026-09-11: Sweden's UNESCO Tentative List holds a single entry (The Rise of Systematic Biology, 2009); Ridanäs is absent. The earlier Wikipedia-based claim is dropped and the site description updated.

  • Herring at the export harbour 2026-08-11

    Frojel harbour, from which Viking-Age goods once shipped out: the same bay now lands herring sold straight at the quayside; one catch at a time, the food corridor comes ashore.

  • The wall's second life 2026-07-19

    Visby, a loop of the town wall: thirteenth-century ramparts now buffer hotels and tour buses; the wall is kept authentic while the life behind it is re-invented for tourism.

Southern Öland

  • Southern Öland boundary includes Gamla Skogsby 2026-09-11

    Desk record, 2026-09-11: UNESCO WHC 968 (criterion iv, v) inscribed area 56,286 ha — confirmed that the property runs from Karlevi to Gårdby and southward (2024 46COM 8D clarifications), placing Gamla Skogsby within the heritage area.

  • From kitchen floor to future grain 2026-08-28

    Gamla Skogsby, on the 2021-season kitchen assemblage: pig bones, quern stones and pottery read as the record of a chieftain household; soil carried out of the floors is being re-sown with heritage cereals.

  • When the past grows new life 2026-08-04

    Gamla Skogsby, Iron Age site: soil, plants and food are being used to ask whether the past can generate future life.

Linköping / Kinda Canal

  • Liveness as starting point: Kinda Canal 2026-09-11

    Desk record, 2026-09-11: kindakanal.se confirms the waterway has been an important route since 1871 (15 hand-operated locks, ~80 km); no official 'living heritage' designation exists — liveness here is the project's premise, not a claim about status.

  • When the canal becomes scenery 2026-09-02

    Nykvarn lock, Lake Roxen: the lock keeper now doubles as guide, freight barges carry day-trippers, and the national-interest designation preserves the 1871 waterway as cultural scenery.
