Living Cultural Corridors

Cultural continuity, connectivity, transformation and regeneration across Beijing, Gotland and Sweden

Human cultures do not live only in monuments, museums, archives or officially designated heritage sites. They live in relationships — in the ways people move, eat, work, trade, remember, make things, inhabit landscapes, care for places, tell stories, use water, cultivate land, build settlements, welcome strangers, adapt to environmental change, and imagine the future.

The project

This field-based, comparative and digitally enabled project investigates how human cultural relations persist, transform and regenerate through movement, place, material life, environment and networks of exchange. The Beijing Grand Canal is the primary field laboratory, Gotland the principal Swedish comparison, with focused studies in Southern Öland and Linköping.

The spotting lens

A particular analytical lens runs through the Beijing case: the subtle interfaces between the Grand Canal and wider Silk Roads systems of movement and exchange.

The deeper ambition

How do human communities carry meaningful relationships from the past into viable futures, while allowing culture itself to change?

Corridors

Grand Canal of Beijing

The principal field laboratory. Imperial history, water infrastructure, urban transformation, food and agricultural flows, trade, and heritage policy converge in Beijing's canal landscape.
Gotland

The principal Swedish comparison — a Baltic island whose Viking Age and Hanseatic past demonstrates intense long-distance connectivity alongside a strong local cultural identity.
Southern Öland

A focused regeneration laboratory — UNESCO-listed agricultural landscape where archaeological soil and food are used to explore how the past generates future life.
Linköping / Kinda Canal

An auxiliary infrastructure comparison — what happens to cultural meaning when an infrastructure loses its original economic function.

Latest Field Observations

  • Ridanäs is not on the tentative list 2026-09-11

    Desk record, 2026-09-11: Sweden's UNESCO Tentative List holds a single entry (The Rise of Systematic Biology, 2009); Ridanäs is absent. The earlier Wikipedia-based claim is dropped and the site description updated.

  • Liveness as starting point: Kinda Canal 2026-09-11

    Desk record, 2026-09-11: kindakanal.se confirms the waterway has been an important route since 1871 (15 hand-operated locks, ~80 km); no official 'living heritage' designation exists — liveness here is the project's premise, not a claim about status.

  • Southern Öland boundary includes Gamla Skogsby 2026-09-11

    Desk record, 2026-09-11: UNESCO WHC 968 (criterion iv, v) inscribed area 56,286 ha — confirmed that the property runs from Karlevi to Gårdby and southward (2024 46COM 8D clarifications), placing Gamla Skogsby within the heritage area.

  • When the canal becomes scenery 2026-09-02

    Nykvarn lock, Lake Roxen: the lock keeper now doubles as guide, freight barges carry day-trippers, and the national-interest designation preserves the 1871 waterway as cultural scenery.

  • From kitchen floor to future grain 2026-08-28

    Gamla Skogsby, on the 2021-season kitchen assemblage: pig bones, quern stones and pottery read as the record of a chieftain household; soil carried out of the floors is being re-sown with heritage cereals.
