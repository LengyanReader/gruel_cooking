Bjørnar Olsen

UiT The Arctic University of Norway

Bjørnar Olsen

UiT The Arctic University of Norway

Archaeology; materiality; contemporary archaeology; ruins

UiT The Arctic University of Norway

Professor of Archaeology, UiT The Arctic University of Norway (since 1994). Contributes materiality, things, contemporary archaeology, ruins, and infrastructures. Key works: In Defense of Things (2010); Archaeology: The Discipline of Things (2012); Ruin Memories (2014); Unruly Heritage (2025). His involvement in the Arctic Silk Road project at UiT examines contemporary infrastructure, anticipated connectivity and local uncertainty across northern regions.

https://uit.no/ansatte/bjornar.olsen

Taste

A taste for the leftovers of modernity — ruins, decommissioned infrastructures, off-season fishing camps — rather than canonical monuments. Contemporary things as the honest face of our own time.

Quality

A theorist who grounds ontology in fieldworn material; co-author of one of the most cited manifestos of symmetrical archaeology; able to write both sharply and simply.

Spirit

Anti-humanist courage — things are not passive background but co-agents of social life; a plea to stop reading everything through the human.

Character

Unsentimental, unpretentious, uncompromising; a steady voice that faces decay, abandonment and the unruly side of heritage without flinching.

Concerns

Modern ruins and material memory; 'unruly heritage' in the Anthropocene; Arctic infrastructures and anticipated connectivity; how things outlast the people who made them.

Philosophy

New materialism / symmetrical ontology: things 'push back', have memory-like endurance, and hold agency in their own right; the past is not only in the mind.
