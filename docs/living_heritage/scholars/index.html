Theoretical constellation

The project works with a constellation of thinkers whose work grounds its epistemology of movement, place, materiality, heritage and regeneration.

Maris Boyd Gillette

University of Gothenburg

Anthropology; China; material culture; food; labour

University of Gothenburg

Professor of Anthropology, School of Global Studies, University of Gothenburg (since 2017). Previously at Haverford College, Pennsylvania. Contributes China, material culture, economic life, labour, sustainability, food and material production, and community and museum engagement. Key works: Between Mecca and Beijing (2000, Stanford UP); China's Porcelain Capital (2016, Bloomsbury); work on mining heritage tourism in China (Journal of Heritage Tourism, 2024, with E. Boyd); on Swedish herring fisheries (Sociologia Ruralis, 2026). Evidence: University of Gothenburg staff records [A].

Cornelius Holtorf

Linnaeus University, Kalmar

Heritage futures; regeneration; future generations

Linnaeus University; photo Johannes Rydström

Professor of Archaeology, Department of Cultural Sciences, Linnaeus University, Kalmar (since 2008); UNESCO Chair on Heritage Futures (since 2017, renewed 2021). Contributes heritage futures, future generations, uncertainty, regeneration, transformation, and stakeholder engagement. Key works: Heritage Futures (Harrison, DeSilvey, Holtorf et al. 2020, UCL Press); Cultural Heritage and the Future (2021, Routledge); 'Embracing Change' (World Archaeology 50(4), 2018); 'An Archaeology of Growth and Regeneration' (Kalmar Studies in Archaeology XIV, 2022, pp. 85-123). Evidence: Linnaeus University staff page [A].

Tim Ingold

University of Aberdeen

Social anthropology; wayfaring; human-environment relations

Photo: FeastFX, CC BY 3.0 / Wikimedia Commons

Emeritus Professor of Social Anthropology, University of Aberdeen (Fellow of the British Academy; CBE 2022). Retired 2018 but remains highly active with publications through 2026 (~109,000 citations). Provides an epistemology of walking, wayfaring, attention, practical knowledge, and human-environment relations. Key works: The Perception of the Environment (2000); Lines: A Brief History (2007); Being Alive (2011); The Life of Lines (2015); Imagining for Real (2022). The field should be a place of knowledge production, not merely data collection.

Helene Martinsson-Wallin

Uppsala University, Campus Gotland

Archaeology; landscape; historical ecology; sustainability

Uppsala University

Professor of Archaeology, Uppsala University, Campus Gotland (30+ years on Gotland). Contributes deep time, archaeology, landscape, historical ecology, sustainability, fisheries, tourism, and long-term human-environment relations. Key works include Martinsson-Wallin 1994 (PhD on Easter Island ahu) and Poort et al. 2021 (Sustainability 13(11): 6302) on Gotland-Rapa Nui comparative cultural tourism. Evidence: Uppsala University staff records [A].

Bjørnar Olsen

UiT The Arctic University of Norway

Archaeology; materiality; contemporary archaeology; ruins

UiT The Arctic University of Norway

Professor of Archaeology, UiT The Arctic University of Norway (since 1994). Contributes materiality, things, contemporary archaeology, ruins, and infrastructures. Key works: In Defense of Things (2010); Archaeology: The Discipline of Things (2012); Ruin Memories (2014); Unruly Heritage (2025). His involvement in the Arctic Silk Road project at UiT examines contemporary infrastructure, anticipated connectivity and local uncertainty across northern regions.
