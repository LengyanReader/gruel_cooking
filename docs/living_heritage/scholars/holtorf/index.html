Cornelius Holtorf

Linnaeus University, Kalmar

Cornelius Holtorf

Linnaeus University, Kalmar

Heritage futures; regeneration; future generations

Linnaeus University; photo Johannes Rydström

Professor of Archaeology, Department of Cultural Sciences, Linnaeus University, Kalmar (since 2008); UNESCO Chair on Heritage Futures (since 2017, renewed 2021). Contributes heritage futures, future generations, uncertainty, regeneration, transformation, and stakeholder engagement. Key works: Heritage Futures (Harrison, DeSilvey, Holtorf et al. 2020, UCL Press); Cultural Heritage and the Future (2021, Routledge); 'Embracing Change' (World Archaeology 50(4), 2018); 'An Archaeology of Growth and Regeneration' (Kalmar Studies in Archaeology XIV, 2022, pp. 85-123). Evidence: Linnaeus University staff page [A].

https://corneliusholtorf.com

Taste

A taste for the counterintuitive question: what will heritage do for people a decade — or a century — from now? The future tense as the most interesting tense in heritage studies.

Quality

A communicator and convenor: UNESCO Chair, multi-partner volumes, plain prose; turns avant-garde frameworks (heritage futures, foresight literacy) into usable, testable practice.

Spirit

Embrace change — heritage is about building capacity for transformation, not freezing the past; optimistic, pragmatic and radically forward-looking.

Character

Open-handed, gregarious and scenario-minded; thrives on dialogue with stakeholders, artists, students and publics rather than monologue.

Concerns

Future generations and uncertainty; heritage standards and financing; nuclear waste as future heritage; regeneration at Gamla Skogsby; climate change and heritage practice.

Philosophy

Heritage-futures: heritage is future-making, not past-restoration; pasts are resources for building viable futures, and uncertainty is a source, not a problem.
