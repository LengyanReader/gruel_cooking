M

Maris Boyd Gillette

University of Gothenburg

Anthropology; China; material culture; food; labour

Professor of Anthropology, School of Global Studies, University of Gothenburg (since 2017). Previously at Haverford College, Pennsylvania. Contributes China, material culture, economic life, labour, sustainability, food and material production, and community and museum engagement. Key works: Between Mecca and Beijing (2000, Stanford UP); China's Porcelain Capital (2016, Bloomsbury); work on mining heritage tourism in China (Journal of Heritage Tourism, 2024, with E. Boyd); on Swedish herring fisheries (Sociologia Ruralis, 2026). Evidence: University of Gothenburg staff records [A].

https://www.gu.se/en/about/find-staff/marisgillette

Taste

A taste for the concrete and the everyday as a window: a bowl, a herring, a coal mine — small tangible things carrying large historical transformations.

Quality

Deep, longitudinal ethnography; bilingual archival and field scholarship across China and Sweden; a willingness to start over in a new field and bring the old skills along.

Spirit

Intellectual humility before material production: take seriously what people make, buy, copy and eat — these are not trivia but the site where capitalism and culture meet.

Character

Quiet, meticulous, patient and unflashy; an ethnographer who lets sources and craft speak rather than performing authority.

Concerns

Labour, fabrication and copying; urban consumption and modernization in China; mining-heritage tourism; Swedish small-scale fisheries and food sustainability.

Philosophy

Materialist anthropology of livelihood: economic life is the strongest tracer of cultural relations; sustainability is a relational question of life, not a label.
