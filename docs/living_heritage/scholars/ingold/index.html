Tim Ingold

University of Aberdeen

Social anthropology; wayfaring; human-environment relations

Emeritus Professor of Social Anthropology, University of Aberdeen (Fellow of the British Academy; CBE 2022). Retired 2018 but remains highly active with publications through 2026 (~109,000 citations). Provides an epistemology of walking, wayfaring, attention, practical knowledge, and human-environment relations. Key works: The Perception of the Environment (2000); Lines: A Brief History (2007); Being Alive (2011); The Life of Lines (2015); Imagining for Real (2022). The field should be a place of knowledge production, not merely data collection.

https://www.timingold.com

Taste

A taste for unobserved everyday things — lines, walking, string, basketweaving — treated as epistemological protagonists rather than examples. Revulsion against reducing the world to objects-of-representation.

Quality

Rare clarity at great depth; wide erudition spanning anthropology, ecology, art and education; concepts (wayfaring, meshwork, attention, correspondence) that travel far beyond one discipline.

Spirit

The field is a place of knowledge production, not data collection; 'not knowing and paying attention' as a method and a virtue; writing as attention to the world's self-revelation.

Character

Gentle, relentless and quietly radical; a teacher-writer who changes his mind in print and asks his readers to keep their lines open and unfinished.

Concerns

Human-environment correspondence, dwelling and livelihood; how attention and practical skills shape knowledge; the education of the next generation; the future of knowledge itself.

Philosophy

Process-relational ontology: things are not objects but ongoing movement-lines; 'being-in-becoming'; life as a meshwork of interwoven, never-finished kinds of growth.
