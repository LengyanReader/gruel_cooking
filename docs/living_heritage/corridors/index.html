Living Cultural Corridors

Research corridors

A corridor is more than a physical route. A canal, road, river, maritime passage, railway or trade path can become a medium through which people, goods, knowledge, technologies, foods, memories, skills and ways of living encounter one another. But the corridor itself is not the culture — culture emerges through the relationships that develop around it.

The comparative architecture

Two cases, one broader phenomenon: how can a place be deeply connected to distant worlds without becoming culturally indistinguishable from them? Beijing and Gotland are treated as different examples of local cultural worlds embedded in extensive networks of exchange.

Grand Canal of Beijing

4 Heritage Sites

The principal field laboratory. Imperial history, water infrastructure, urban transformation, food and agricultural flows, trade, and heritage policy converge in Beijing's canal landscape.

Grand Canal (Beijing Section)

World Heritage (C 1443, 2014)

Principal field laboratory. UNESCO describes the Grand Canal (C 1443, 2014) in terms of transportation, political and economic integration and cultural interchange. The Beijing section is studied as a deep field laboratory within the larger canal system, with attention to canal-road, canal-river and canal-city interfaces. Evidence: UNESCO WHC C 1443 [A].

Shichahai–Yuhe Historic Area

Within Grand Canal setting

A principal Beijing site for investigating the chain water → urban life → food → markets → residence → memory → tourism. Questions include: What activities remain connected to water? What has been reinvented? Which routes remain socially meaningful? Who uses these spaces? Evidence: drafted research proposal [B]; historical and urban studies [B].

Tongzhou Canal Frontier

Within Grand Canal setting

A second major Beijing field environment. The canal is explicitly implicated in contemporary urban development, public space, cultural programming and regeneration. Research question: What happens to a historical transportation corridor when it becomes an instrument of contemporary urban regeneration? Evidence: drafted research proposal [B].

urban_regeneration Ming Dynasty

Zhangjiawan Canal Gateway

Within Grand Canal setting

Historically a canal gateway environment where goods transferred between water and land routes. Research question: What becomes visible when we follow movement rather than monuments? Evidence: drafted research proposal [B].

Gotland

2 Heritage Sites

The principal Swedish comparison — a Baltic island whose Viking Age and Hanseatic past demonstrates intense long-distance connectivity alongside a strong local cultural identity.

Ridanäs (Fröjel)

Not on UNESCO Tentative List (verified 2026-09-11)

Viking Age trading port on Gotland demonstrating long-distance connectivity, with imported materials from mainland Sweden, Italy, Poland/Lithuania and the Caucasus (Carlsson 1999; Peschel et al. 2017). Isotope evidence indicates ~85% of studied individuals were local (Peschel et al. 2017, Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports 13: 175-184). Ridanäs is NOT on the UNESCO Tentative List (Sweden's list holds one entry, 'The Rise of Systematic Biology', 2009) [A: whc.unesco.org].

archaeological Viking Age (c. 8th–11th century)

Hanseatic Town of Visby

World Heritage (731, 1995)

UNESCO World Heritage (Reference 731, 1995), described as 'the best-preserved fortified commercial city in northern Europe'. Main centre of the Hanseatic League in the Baltic from the 12th to 14th century. Provides the urban and maritime context for investigating historical connectivity, medieval identity, heritage, commerce, tourism and contemporary representations of the past. Evidence: UNESCO WHC 731 [A].

Southern Öland

1 Heritage Sites

A focused regeneration laboratory — UNESCO-listed agricultural landscape where archaeological soil and food are used to explore how the past generates future life.

Gamla Skogsby

Within World Heritage (968, 2000)

Iron Age settlement (c. AD 400-550) within the UNESCO-listed Agricultural Landscape of Southern Öland (Reference 968, 2000). Excavations led by Ludvig Papmehl-Dufay (2019-ongoing) revealed a chieftain's estate with 19 house foundations, extensive food-preparation evidence and pig-bone assemblages associated with feasting (Papmehl-Dufay 2020-2025). Holtorf's chapter 'An Archaeology of Growth and Regeneration' (2022) connects this evidence to the past generating future life. Boundary verified 2026-09-11: the inscribed property (56,286 ha) runs from Karlevi to Gårdby and southward (2024 46COM 8D clarifications) — Gamla Skogsby lies within the heritage area [A: whc.unesco.org].

archaeological c. AD 400-550

Linköping / Kinda Canal

1 Heritage Sites

An auxiliary infrastructure comparison — what happens to cultural meaning when an infrastructure loses its original economic function.

Kinda Canal

National interest designation (riksintresse)

Completed 1871, historically connecting southern forest-producing areas with northern agricultural areas. Now survives partly through cultural and tourism functions and is designated a 'kulturhistoriskt värdefull miljö av riksintresse' (culturally valuable environment of national interest). Connects to the Göta Canal system via Lake Roxen (Nykvarn lock). Evidence: Linköpings kommun; kindakanal.se [B]; drafted research proposal [B]. Liveness verified 2026-09-11: no official 'living heritage' designation exists — the canal has been an important route since 1871 (15 hand locks, ~80 km); 'living' is the project's starting point, not a status claim.
