# Ruben Laukkonen — Research Fields and Circles

## Snapshot

Ruben E. Laukkonen is a cognitive neuroscientist working at the junction of three areas that rarely share a lab: the phenomenology of meditation (including its most extreme states), the mathematics of predictive processing/active inference, and — as of 2025–2026 — the alignment of artificial intelligence with contemplative principles. He describes himself as obsessed with understanding the brain, consciousness, artificial intelligence, and meditation, and believes wisdom traditions hold some of the keys to aligning AI with human flourishing. Unusually for a scientist in this space, he is also a practitioner with formal lineage: trained in Zen and authorized to teach Theravada vipassana, which visibly shapes both his research questions and his choice of collaborators.

## Career trajectory

- Completed an award-winning PhD on the cognitive science of insight at the University of Queensland in 2019, work done in **Jason Tangen's** lab (later listed by ResearchGate as his "Senior Researcher" home lab).
- Moved to postdoctoral research at the Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam on the computational neuroscience of meditation, working with **Heleen Slagter**.
- Built his own research group at **Southern Cross University** (Gold Coast, Australia), Faculty of Health, as a Lecturer from June 2022, where his lab worked on empirically grounded and experientially authentic models of meditation, insight, psychedelics, and consciousness.
- Holds honorary fellowships at both Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam and the University of Queensland.
- Now based primarily at the **University of Oxford**, where by 2025–2026 he holds several concurrent roles: Senior Researcher within Oxford's Department of Psychiatry, director of the Flourishing Intelligence Program (FLIP), co-director of the Centre for Eudaimonia and Human Flourishing, and Fellow of Linacre College. Southern Cross University's Faculty of Health is still listed as his affiliation on the 2025 "Beautiful Loop" paper, so the two appointments appear to overlap rather than being a clean handoff.
- Consults for the Organisation of Economic Co-operation and Development and speaks regularly at international conferences.
- Before the switch into psychology and neuroscience, he competed semi-professionally in Muay Thai kickboxing and co-founded two businesses, including the first online Bitcoin marketplace in Australia.

## Research programme

### 1. The cognitive science of insight ("Aha!" moments)

His oldest and most sustained line of work.

- Methodology: "How to detect insight moments in problem solving experiments" (Laukkonen & Tangen, 2018, *Frontiers in Psychology*).
- "The dark side of Eureka: Artificially induced Aha moments make facts feel true" (Laukkonen, Kaveladze, Tangen & Schooler, 2020, *Cognition*) and "Getting a grip on insight: real-time and embodied Aha experiences predict correct solutions" (2021, *Cognition and Emotion*).
- Boundary conditions: "Thinking style and psychosis proneness do not predict false insights" (Grimmer, Laukkonen et al., 2022) and "The illusion of insight: detailed warnings reduce but do not prevent false Aha moments" (2023).
- Theoretical synthesis: "Insight and the Selection of Ideas" (Laukkonen, Webb, Salvi, Tangen, Slagter & Schooler, 2023, Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews) proposes that feelings of insight play a central role in heuristically selecting an idea from the stream of consciousness, by capturing attention and producing a sense of intuitive confidence.
- **Circle:** Jason Tangen (UQ, his long-running senior collaborator), Jonathan Schooler (UC Santa Barbara — mind-wandering/meta-awareness), Carola Salvi (now Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore, Milan), Hilary Grimmer and William von Hippel (UQ), Benjamin Kaveladze (Northwestern), Margaret Webb, Anna Freydenzon, John Protzko.

### 2. Predictive processing & active inference models of meditation

- Flagship paper: "From many to (n)one: Meditation and the plasticity of the predictive mind" (Laukkonen & Slagter, 2021, *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*). It maps meditation techniques onto operations within a hierarchical predictive brain — arguing that different styles work by turning down the "precision" assigned to different levels of the mind's models (sensory/attentional priors in focused attention, conceptual priors in open monitoring, the self-model itself in non-dual practice). The title's pun gestures at both destinations: non-dual union ("one") and, at the limit, no experience at all ("none").
- Empirical follow-through: "Clear Mind: Meditation and the Brain's Signal-to-Noise Ratio" (2026) and "Meditation depth enhances the functional signal-to-noise ratio of the brain" (Nath, Reggente, Bailey, Kringelbach & Laukkonen, 2026), which try to operationalise the "precision" story as a measurable neural signal-to-noise metric.
- **Circle:** Heleen Slagter (VU Amsterdam — recurring co-author across nearly every meditation paper), Morten Kringelbach (Oxford/Aarhus), Manesh Nath, Nicco Reggente, Nathan Bailey.

### 3. Cessation of consciousness — *nirodha samāpatti*

The study that has drawn the most public attention.

- "Cessations of Consciousness in Meditation: Advancing a Scientific Understanding of Nirodha Samāpatti" (Laukkonen, Sacchet, Barendregt, Devaney, Chowdhury & Slagter, 2023, Progress in Brain Research) documents the classical Buddhist attainment in which advanced meditators report a total, extended absence of consciousness, distinct from unconsciousness caused by injury, anaesthesia, or fainting, and lasting up to seven days by some practitioner accounts.
- The central case is **Delson Armstrong**, a meditation teacher authorized to teach Tranquil Wisdom Insight Meditation (TWIM) in the American Forest Buddhist tradition by Bhante Vimalaramsi, whose self-timed cessations were studied with EEG in Slagter's lab. Public dialogues about the study, hosted on the Guru Viking podcast, also brought in meditation teacher Shinzen Young and Columbia-trained neuroscientist Chelsey Fasano. The finding drew real interest but also some debate within Buddhist practice communities about reliability and interpretation.
- **Circle:** Matthew Sacchet (Meditation Research Program, Dept. of Psychiatry, Harvard Medical School / Massachusetts General Hospital), Henk Barendregt (Radboud University Nijmegen — also Laukkonen's own meditation teacher, see below), Kathryn Devaney (UC Berkeley Center for the Science of Psychedelics), Avijit Chowdhury, and again Heleen Slagter.

### 4. An active-inference theory of consciousness — "A Beautiful Loop"

- "A beautiful loop: An active inference theory of consciousness" (Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria, 2025, Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews) — co-authored with **Karl Friston** (UCL), originator of the free-energy principle. Arguably his most theoretically ambitious paper.
- It proposes three conditions for active inference to support consciousness: a system must simulate a world model rich enough to constitute a knowable "epistemic field"; competing inferences must undergo a selection process the paper calls "Bayesian binding" to enter that model; and the system needs "epistemic depth" — a recurrent sharing of beliefs throughout the hierarchy such that the world model comes to contain the knowledge that it exists, forming the self-referential "loop" of the title. The paper explicitly extends this to altered states like meditation, psychedelics, and minimal states of consciousness, and proposes it as a path toward more flexible, general intelligence.
- The paper circulated through several preprint versions (OSF/PsyArXiv, June–August 2025) before the September 2025 NBR publication.

### 5. Psychedelics and false insight

- "An Integrated theory of false insights and beliefs under psychedelics" (McGovern, Grimmer, Doss, Hutchinson, Timmermann, Lyon, … Laukkonen, 2024, Communications Psychology) extends the insight-as-heuristic framework from strand #1 to explain why psychedelic states so often produce compelling but false "revelations."
- **Circle:** Christopher Timmermann (University College London, psychedelics research), Manoj Doss, Heather McGovern, Brandon Hutchinson, Aidan Lyon.

### 6. Contemplative AI and alignment through wisdom traditions

The newest strand (2025–2026), and by far the widest in collaborators and institutions.

- "Contemplative Artificial Intelligence" (arXiv, 2025) and its peer-reviewed form, "Contemplative Superalignment" (in Artificial General Intelligence, Springer, 2025), propose building a "Wise World Model" into AI around four principles: mindfulness enabling self-monitoring of emergent subgoals, emptiness forestalling dogmatic goal fixation, and non-duality dissolving adversarial self-other boundaries (plus a fourth, boundless care). The authors report gains on the AILuminate safety benchmark and increased cooperation on the Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma task when models were prompted to reflect on these principles.
- "Positive Alignment: Artificial Intelligence for Human Flourishing" (2026 arXiv preprint) broadens this into a large cross-institutional position paper linking AI alignment to the science of well-being.
- **Circle** (unusually wide):
  - **Shamil Chandaria** — the connective figure across nearly all of these papers. Cambridge- and LSE-trained in mathematical modelling of financial systems, with an MA in philosophy from UCL; senior research fellow at Oxford's Centre for Eudaimonia and Human Flourishing, plus fellowships at Imperial College's Centre for Psychedelic Research (which he co-founded), the Institute of Philosophy at the University of London, and Fitzwilliam College, Cambridge; also linked to Oxford's Global Priorities Institute and formerly the Future of Humanity Institute.
  - **Morten Kringelbach** — founding director of the Centre for Eudaimonia and Human Flourishing at Linacre College, focused on hedonia and eudaimonia through brain dynamics, professor of neuroscience at Oxford and Aarhus.
  - **Jakob Hohwy** and **Lars Sandved-Smith** — Monash Centre for Consciousness and Contemplative Studies, Melbourne; Hohwy is one of the best-known philosophers of predictive processing.
  - **Jonathan Gold** — Department of Religion, Princeton University. Worth flagging specifically: he's a scholar of Indian and Tibetan Buddhist philosophy and author of Paving the Great Way: Vasubandhu's Unifying Buddhist Philosophy — an actual academic Yogācāra specialist sitting inside an AI-alignment author list.
  - Edmundo Lopez-Sola (Neuroelectrics, Barcelona / Centre for Brain and Cognition, Universitat Pompeu Fabra), Fionn Inglis (University of Amsterdam), Adam Elwood (Aily Labs, Munich).
  - The "Positive Alignment" paper crosses into industry: Seb Krier, Nenad Tomašev, Matija Franklin, Stephanie Chan, Verena Rieser and Roma Patel (Google DeepMind); Chloé Bakalar (OpenAI); Daniel Ford (Anthropic); Fernando Rosas (University of Sussex); Michael Levin (Tufts University); Maty Bohacek (Stanford); and Arun Rao (UCLA / Positive AI Labs).

### 7. Brain dynamics, flourishing, and mental-health prediction

- "A canary in the mind: A single baseline brain scan predicts adolescent depression and anxiety one year later" (Deco, Sanz Perl, Vohryzek, Garcia-Guzman, Greenstein, Pizzagalli, Laukkonen, Chandaria & Kringelbach, 2026) ties the group's brain-dynamics work to psychiatric risk prediction.
- **Circle:** Gustavo Deco, Yonatan Sanz Perl, Jakub Vohryzek (Centre for Brain and Cognition, Universitat Pompeu Fabra), Diego Pizzagalli (Harvard).

**The practice lineage behind the science** — this functions as a real circle, not just biography:

- Zen: trained under **Gregg Howard**, Guiding Teacher of Ordinary Mind Zen Brisbane, who received permission to teach from Charlotte Joko Beck in 1996 and Dharma transmission in 2004 — Joko Beck herself having been a Dharma heir of Hakuyu Taizan Maezumi Roshi.

Barendregt authorized Laukkonen to teach vipassana in 2024.
