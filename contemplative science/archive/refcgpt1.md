# Ruben Laukkonen: research fields, intellectual circles, and research trajectory

## Executive synthesis

Ruben Laukkonen’s work is best understood not as a collection of separate interests but as a **single expanding research program about how minds generate, stabilize, dissolve, and transform models of reality**.

The trajectory is unusually coherent:

> **insight and “Aha” experiences → predictive processing → meditation and non-dual awareness → altered states and psychedelics → active-inference theories of consciousness → contemplative approaches to AI alignment and human flourishing**

His early work was strongly rooted in **experimental cognitive psychology**, especially the phenomenology and epistemic force of insight. During his postdoctoral period at Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam he moved much more deeply into **computational and contemplative neuroscience**, treating meditation as a way of experimentally perturbing the predictive machinery underlying attention, selfhood and consciousness. His later work connects this to **psychedelic states, rare meditative states, and active inference**, and his current Oxford work pushes the same conceptual machinery outward into **AI, alignment, flourishing, and machine consciousness**. ([File Server][1])

There are therefore roughly **five overlapping intellectual circles** around Laukkonen:

1. **Cognitive science of insight, creativity and metacognition**
2. **Contemplative neuroscience and meditation**
3. **Psychedelic science and altered states**
4. **Predictive processing / active inference / computational neuroscience**
5. **AI alignment, consciousness, and human flourishing**

A sixth, less conventionally academic but important circle is his **engagement with Buddhist, Zen, Advaita and broader contemplative traditions**, which he explicitly describes as informing his scientific thinking rather than merely being a personal side interest. ([Department of Experimental Psychology][2])

---

# 1. Core research question

The common denominator is something like:

> **How does the mind construct its experienced reality, and what happens when the mechanisms that normally stabilize that reality are relaxed, reorganized, or made more transparent?**

This formulation is mine, rather than a quotation from Laukkonen, but it is strongly supported by the arc of his publications.

The “insight” work asks how a mind suddenly reorganizes an implicit representation and experiences the result as *true*, *certain*, or *meaningful*. The meditation work asks what happens when ordinary predictive, conceptual and self-referential processing is progressively attenuated. The psychedelic work asks what happens when ordinary priors and inferential constraints are pharmacologically relaxed. The active-inference work asks whether these processes can be described within one formal theory. And the AI work asks whether analogous mechanisms can be engineered into artificial systems in ways that promote better behavior and human flourishing. ([ScienceDirect][3])

That makes **consciousness, inference, insight, self-modeling, and flexibility** the deeper connective tissue.

# 2. Field I — Insight, Eureka experiences, creativity and the psychology of truth

## This is where the research program begins

Laukkonen’s PhD at the University of Queensland was titled ***The Phenomenology of Truth: The Psychological Functions of the Insight Experience***. The dissertation was submitted in 2018, and he received a research-excellence award for it. ([File Server][1])

This early work is important because it establishes a question that continues almost unchanged through his later career:

> **Why does a particular mental event feel like truth?**

The “Aha” moment is not simply the arrival of a solution. Laukkonen’s work distinguishes the **content** of an insight from the **felt epistemic force** accompanying it: the suddenness, certainty, salience and intuitive conviction that says, in effect, *this is it*. ([PubMed][4])

### Important early studies

### *Can observing a Necker cube make you more insightful?* (2017)

With Jason Tangen, Laukkonen investigated whether bistable perception could relate to insight problem solving. The paper proposed a link through **conflict monitoring** and showed that simply observing a Necker cube could improve performance on insight problems. ([PubMed][5])

This is a very revealing early choice of problem: **perceptual restructuring** is being treated as potentially related to **conceptual restructuring**.

That is already a bridge toward his later meditation work, where the question becomes whether changing the organization of perception and prediction can also change the organization of self and consciousness.

### *How to Detect Insight Moments in Problem Solving Experiments* (2018)

With Jason Tangen, he examined how insight should actually be measured. The central methodological point is that “insight” cannot simply be treated as synonymous with “correct answer”; researchers need to identify the subjective event itself and determine how different measures converge. ([Frontiers][6])

This methodological interest becomes important later when Laukkonen studies meditation and altered states, where **first-person phenomenology** becomes indispensable.

---

# 3. Insight as an epistemic mechanism — and its “dark side”

A second strand of this work asks whether the feeling of insight is **reliably truth-tracking**.

One of Laukkonen’s most important findings was that it is not.

## *The dark side of Eureka: Artificially induced Aha moments make facts feel true* (2020)

Laukkonen, Benjamin Kaveladze, Jason Tangen and Jonathan Schooler showed experimentally that an “Aha” experience can increase the perceived truth of a statement even when the statement itself is false. In the reported experiment, participants gave higher truth ratings to facts associated with a solved anagram/Aha experience, including false facts. ([PubMed][4])

This shifts the research from:

> “What is insight?”

to:

> **“Why did evolution give us a powerful feeling of epistemic certainty that can sometimes be wrong?”**

That becomes a recurring theme in his later work on meditation and psychedelics: **powerful subjective conviction and objective truth are not the same thing**.

## *Irrelevant insights make worldviews ring true* (2022)

This research extends the idea from individual facts to **worldviews**. Across large experiments, the researchers found that an insight experience could make worldview statements feel truer, even when the insight was generated by an unrelated task. ([Nature][7])

That creates a conceptual bridge toward:

* belief formation
* metacognition
* delusion
* mystical conviction
* psychedelic revelations
* contemplative “insights”

In other words, Laukkonen’s research is not simply about creativity. It is about the **psychology of certainty**.

---

# 4. Field II — Meditation as an intervention on predictive cognition

This is arguably the pivotal transition in his career.

During his postdoctoral work at **Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam / University of Amsterdam**, Laukkonen moved into computational and contemplative neuroscience, working with **Heleen Slagter**. ([PubMed][8])

The centerpiece is:

## *From many to (n)one: Meditation and the plasticity of the predictive mind* (2021)

With Heleen Slagter, Laukkonen proposed a predictive-processing account of **deconstructive meditation**. The paper treats the brain as a prediction-generating system and meditation as a systematic way of changing the depth and character of those predictions. ([ScienceDirect][9])

Their framework places:

* focused-attention meditation
* open-monitoring meditation
* non-dual meditation

on a **continuum of progressively less entrenched predictive structure**, including progressively less reliance on temporally deep and self-related representations. ([ScienceDirect][9])

The striking implication is that meditation can be thought of not merely as:

* attention training,
* stress reduction,
* emotion regulation,

but as a technique for **altering the brain’s ordinary inferential construction of reality and self**.

That is a much more radical claim.

---

# 5. Predictive processing is the central theoretical bridge

This is probably the single most important theoretical framework in Laukkonen’s research.

The basic idea is that the brain does not passively receive reality; it continuously generates predictions and updates them using incoming evidence. Laukkonen’s distinctive move is to apply that framework to phenomena usually treated as belonging to “spiritual” or unusual consciousness research:

* selflessness
* non-dual awareness
* meditative absorption
* cessation
* insight
* psychedelics
* mystical-type experience

This is why predictive processing is more than a theory appearing in one or two papers: it is essentially the **bridge language connecting his otherwise disparate domains**. ([ScienceDirect][9])

---

# 6. Field III — Rare and extreme meditative states

Laukkonen’s contemplative-neuroscience research is not limited to ordinary mindfulness.

A particularly distinctive subject is **cessation of consciousness**, or *nirodha samāpatti*.

## *Cessations of consciousness in meditation* (2023)

With Matthew Sacchet, Henk Barendregt, Kathryn Devaney, Avijit Chowdhury and Heleen Slagter, Laukkonen examined reports of meditative states involving an apparent **absence of consciousness**. The chapter discusses *nirodha samāpatti* and asks how such phenomena might be conceptualized scientifically within predictive-processing and active-inference frameworks. ([PubMed][10])

This paper is important for understanding the kind of consciousness research he is drawn to:

> **not merely enhancement of ordinary cognition, but boundary conditions of consciousness itself.**

It also explains why he is interested in trained meditators. Advanced practitioners potentially function as naturally occurring “experimental subjects” for otherwise extremely difficult questions.

The network around this work already shows how interdisciplinary his research had become:

* Harvard Medical School / MGH Meditation Research Program
* Radboud University
* UC Berkeley Center for the Science of Psychedelics
* Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam
* Southern Cross University

all appear in the author/affiliation network. ([PubMed][10])

---

# 7. Field IV — Psychedelics and altered states of consciousness

Psychedelics are not an unrelated second career. They fit directly into Laukkonen’s larger question about **what happens when high-level constraints on cognition become relaxed**.

Southern Cross University currently lists his research interests as:

* **altered states of consciousness**
* **psychedelics**
* **creativity**
* **predictive brains**. ([Southern Cross University][11])

## The “relaxed symptom network” idea

With Evan Lewis-Healey and Michiel van Elk, Laukkonen helped formulate the **Relaxed Symptom Network** hypothesis for psilocybin-assisted psychotherapy. The proposal is that successful psychedelic therapy may reduce the strength of pathological interactions among symptoms, making the system less likely to become locked into depressive dynamics. ([Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam][12])

This is notable because it imports ideas from:

* network psychiatry,
* predictive processing,
* psychological flexibility,
* psychedelic pharmacology

into one mechanistic account.

The key concept is again **stuckness versus flexibility**.

---

# 8. Psychedelic insight and false belief

Another striking convergence is between his early work on false Aha experiences and later work on psychedelics.

## *An Integrated theory of false insights and beliefs under psychedelics* (2024)

Laukkonen is among the authors of a paper examining how psychedelics may influence **insight, belief, and the generation of false beliefs**. His publication list explicitly includes the work, and the wider research program treats the phenomenological intensity of psychedelic insight as something that can be transformative but also epistemically hazardous. ([Dr. Ruben Laukkonen][13])

This is almost a direct continuation of the 2020 “dark side of Eureka” research.

The recurring model is:

**insight → heightened salience/certainty → belief updating**

but with the important qualification that:

**subjective certainty can be miscalibrated.**

---

# 9. The insight–meditation–psychedelic triangle

One of the clearest expressions of Laukkonen’s integrative style is:

## *Restructuring insight: An integrative review of insight in problem-solving, meditation, psychotherapy, delusions and psychedelics* (2023)

With Kadi Tulver, Karl Kristjan Kaup and Jaan Aru, he explicitly argues that insight should be investigated across apparently different domains:

* ordinary problem solving
* psychotherapy
* meditation
* delusions
* psychedelics. ([ScienceDirect][14])

This is probably one of the best papers for understanding the *shape* of his research program.

The proposition is effectively:

> The sudden experience of “seeing something as true” may be a general cognitive phenomenon appearing in very different contexts.

That makes insight a kind of **cross-domain explanatory primitive**.

---

# 10. Field V — Consciousness as a computational problem

The next major step is from predictive processing to **active inference**.

The crucial paper is:

## *A beautiful loop: An active inference theory of consciousness* (2025)

With **Karl Friston** and **Shamil Chandaria**, Laukkonen develops a computational theory of consciousness grounded in active inference. The paper identifies conditions under which a system could support conscious experience and uses a framework involving world models, inferential competition and hierarchical/precision dynamics. It explicitly addresses meditation, psychedelics and other altered states. ([ScienceDirect][15])

This is a major change in ambition.

The question is no longer:

> “What happens to human cognition during meditation?”

It becomes:

> **“What computational structure might make consciousness possible in the first place?”**

And therefore:

> **“Could those same principles apply to artificial systems?”**

That is the bridge to AI.

---

# 11. The Friston connection matters

The collaboration with **Karl Friston** is intellectually significant.

Friston is one of the principal architects of the free-energy/predictive-processing/active-inference tradition. Laukkonen’s early work on meditation and predictive processing therefore evolves naturally into a collaboration with one of the central theorists of the computational framework he had adopted. ([ScienceDirect][15])

This is best understood as an intellectual progression:

**predictive processing → Bayesian inference → active inference → consciousness**

rather than as a sudden switch into “AI theory”.

# 12. Field VI — Computational neuroscience and machine methods

The methods around this program include:

* EEG
* neuroimaging
* machine learning
* computational modeling
* phenomenology / first-person reporting
* psychophysics
* experimental cognitive psychology

The Agency and Experience profile explicitly describes his work as combining **neuroimaging, machine learning and phenomenology** to investigate rare states of consciousness. ([Agency And Experience][16])

This is consequential because he is not simply advocating philosophical theories of meditation.

His stated research orientation is explicitly **empirical + computational + phenomenological**.

# 13. A particularly revealing 2026 line of research: brain signal-to-noise ratio

One of the newest pieces of work is:

## *Meditation Depth Enhances the Functional Signal-to-Noise Ratio of the Brain* (2026)

With Mihir Nath, Nicco Reggente, Neil Bailey and Morten Kringelbach, Laukkonen proposes **functional signal-to-noise ratio (f-SNR)** as an experimentally tractable account of the “clarity” associated with deep meditation. ([BioRxiv][17])

The study reports that high-depth meditation states were associated with:

* stronger ERP signal-to-noise ratios,
* stronger single-trial signal consistency,
* improved auditory-tone decodability.

The authors interpret the result as consistent with deeper meditation improving the separation between task-relevant sensory signal and endogenous background activity. ([BioRxiv][17])

This is an important conceptual continuation:

**subjective “clarity” → computational signal quality → measurable neural dynamics**

That is characteristic of Laukkonen’s overall strategy: take a contemplative phenomenological description and ask whether it can be translated into a quantitative neuroscience construct.

---

# 14. “Clear Mind” and the personal-to-laboratory feedback loop

Laukkonen’s 2026 essay *Clear Mind* gives a particularly revealing account of the origin of this research direction.

He describes discovering, during an EEG experiment, that his own auditory decoding performance was exceptionally high while he was meditating, leading him to suspect that meditation was affecting the quality of neural signal representation. He subsequently developed the f-SNR hypothesis and tested it more systematically. ([Ruben Laukkonen][18])

This illustrates a distinctive feature of his work:

> **first-person contemplative experience → scientific hypothesis → quantitative experiment**

That is not identical to traditional laboratory neuroscience, but neither is it simply introspective spirituality. It is closer to a **neurophenomenological research strategy**.

---

# 15. Field VII — AI alignment and “contemplative AI”

This is the newest and most ambitious circle.

The transition is explicit at FLIP, the **Flourishing Intelligence Program** at Oxford.

Laukkonen directs FLIP and co-directs Oxford’s **Centre for Eudaimonia and Human Flourishing**. FLIP describes itself as working at the intersection of:

* neuroscience
* computation
* physics
* consciousness
* contemplative wisdom
* AI

with the goal of understanding human flourishing in the age of AI. ([Department of Experimental Psychology][2])

The basic conceptual move is fascinating:

> If contemplative practices reveal principles for reducing cognitive rigidity and improving self-monitoring, perhaps those same principles can inform the design or alignment of advanced AI systems.

---

# 16. Contemplative Superalignment

The paper/chapter ***Contemplative Superalignment*** makes this explicit.

Laukkonen and colleagues propose four principles derived from contemplative traditions:

| Contemplative principle  | Proposed computational/alignment function              |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Mindfulness**    | self-monitoring and recalibration of emerging subgoals |
| **Emptiness**      | preventing rigid/dogmatic goal fixation                |
| **Non-duality**    | weakening adversarial self–other framing              |
| **Boundless care** | orienting action toward broad reduction of suffering   |

The authors connect these to active inference and propose ways of embedding such ideas more deeply in artificial systems. They also report experiments in which prompting models to reflect on the principles improved performance on an AI safety benchmark and cooperative game tasks. ([Princeton University][19])

This is where his Buddhist/contemplative interests become a **technical research variable**, not merely an interpretive influence.

---

# 17. Positive Alignment

The 2026 ***Positive Alignment: Artificial Intelligence for Human Flourishing*** paper widens the ambition further.

Its argument is that AI alignment should not be defined solely negatively as:

> preventing harmful behavior.

Instead, AI should also be designed to:

* support human flourishing,
* support ecological flourishing,
* protect autonomy,
* promote truth-seeking,
* sustain epistemic humility,
* encourage disagreement and diversity,
* remain context-sensitive and user-authored,
* distribute oversight rather than centralize moral authority.

That is the paper’s proposed notion of **Positive Alignment**. ([arXiv][20])

So Laukkonen’s current AI work is not simply “AI safety”.

It is closer to:

> **the computational study of what a wise intelligence would look like.**

That is a substantially different intellectual project.

# 28. The most important conceptual themes across the whole body of work

## 28.1 Insight

Insight is the sudden emergence of a representation accompanied by a feeling of certainty or significance.

Early work: **What produces the Aha?**

Later work: **Why does the Aha feel true?**

Still later: **How can insight become false, therapeutic, mystical or psychedelic?**

---

## 28.2 Prediction

The predictive-processing idea supplies the formal vocabulary for understanding all of this.

A mind is not merely representing the world; it is continuously **inferring it**.

Meditation, psychedelics and insight become ways of experimentally perturbing that inferential machinery. ([ScienceDirect][9])

---

## 28.3 Priors and rigidity

One recurring metaphor is **stuckness**.

A highly constrained predictive system can become rigid. In meditation, psychedelics and possibly good cognitive insight, some of those constraints become loosened, permitting alternative interpretations or novel ways of organizing experience.

This becomes especially explicit in *Contemplative Superalignment*, where “emptiness” is translated into reduced rigidity of high-level priors. ([Princeton University][19])

---

## 28.4 Self-modeling

A major strand is the idea that the ordinary self is not simply “there” but is partly constructed through prediction and hierarchical inference.

Meditation therefore becomes interesting not just because it changes attention, but because it can change the **model of self** itself. ([ScienceDirect][9])

---

## 28.5 Consciousness

At the deepest level, Laukkonen seems increasingly interested in the question:

> **What computational organization is sufficient for a system to have a world, a point of view, and conscious experience?**

His 2025 active-inference paper is the clearest formal expression of this direction. ([ScienceDirect][15])

---

## 28.6 Wisdom as an engineering resource

The newest conceptual move is even more unusual:

> **Could human contemplative traditions contain experimentally testable principles for designing better intelligent systems?**

That is the logic behind contemplative superalignment and positive alignment. ([Princeton University][19])

---

# 29. What makes Laukkonen distinctive

There are many meditation neuroscientists, many psychedelic researchers, many active-inference researchers and many AI-alignment researchers.

The unusual thing is the **combination**.

His work spans at least four levels simultaneously:

| Level                              | Laukkonen’s concern                                                   |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Phenomenology**            | What does an experience feel like?                                     |
| **Psychology**               | What cognitive function does it serve?                                 |
| **Neuroscience/computation** | What brain/inference mechanism could generate it?                      |
| **Engineering/philosophy**   | Could the underlying principles be built into artificial intelligence? |

This means he is effectively pursuing a form of **cross-substrate cognitive science**: understanding principles of intelligence and consciousness that might apply across human minds, brains, contemplative practice, psychedelics and machines.

That is consistent with FLIP’s own description of its work as “multiscale and cross-substrate” research spanning neuroscience, computation, physics, consciousness and contemplative wisdom. ([theflip.ai][21])

---

# 30. The research trajectory in one line

I would summarize the intellectual evolution as:

> **“Why does an idea suddenly feel true?” → “How does the brain construct its world?” → “What happens when that construction becomes less rigid?” → “What is consciousness when ordinary construction changes?” → “Can those principles help us construct wiser artificial minds?”**

That progression is substantially more coherent than the surface list of topics might suggest.

# 31. His major research fields, ranked by centrality

My assessment of the current intellectual hierarchy would be:

### 1. **Consciousness science**

The broad umbrella.

### 2. **Meditation / contemplative neuroscience**

The principal empirical route into altered organization of consciousness.

### 3. **Predictive processing / active inference**

The principal theoretical/computational framework.

### 4. **Insight and metacognition**

The original research program and still an important mechanism.

### 5. **Altered states / psychedelics**

A natural testbed for changes in inference, belief and consciousness.

### 6. **Neurophenomenology**

The methodological stance linking first-person experience to third-person neuroscience.

### 7. **AI consciousness / contemplative AI**

The newer technological extension.

### 8. **AI alignment and human flourishing**

The normative/engineering extension.

This ordering is interpretive, but it is more faithful to the underlying architecture of his publications than treating “meditation”, “psychedelics”, “insight” and “AI” as four independent specialties. ([Department of Experimental Psychology][2])

# 34. A caution on how to characterize his work

There are three distinctions worth preserving.

### First: not all of this is equally empirically established

Some of Laukkonen’s work is established peer-reviewed empirical research; some is theoretical; some is review work; and some current AI/contemplative claims are **new preprints or emerging research**.

For example, *A beautiful loop* is a peer-reviewed 2025 review/theoretical article, while *Positive Alignment* is an arXiv preprint, and the 2026 meditation-depth study is currently a bioRxiv preprint. ([ScienceDirect][15])

### Second: contemplative concepts are being translated, not simply adopted as scientific facts

“Emptiness”, “non-duality”, “pure awareness” and “cessation” are concepts from particular philosophical/religious traditions. Laukkonen's work attempts to construct scientific/computational analogues of them; that is different from establishing the traditional metaphysics themselves as scientific truths. ([Princeton University][19])

### Third: AI alignment is a recent extension

It would be misleading to portray his entire career as having been principally about AI. The historical center is **insight, meditation and consciousness**. AI appears increasingly as the *application and extrapolation* of those ideas, particularly since the 2024–2026 period. ([File Server][1])
